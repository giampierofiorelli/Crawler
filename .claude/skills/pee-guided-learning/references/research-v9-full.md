## 1. Executive intent — what we are really building

The ultimate objective is **an exceptionally competent, independent product-buying adviser**, not merely a search scraper, product catalogue, ranking formula, data warehouse or conversational interface.

A person should be able to say, in everyday English, *“I want something to make excellent chips for just myself; I don't know what features matter.”* PEE should work out what the user is trying to achieve, ask only genuinely useful clarifying questions, learn or retrieve the relevant category knowledge, identify suitable products, locate and appraise independent evidence, and produce its **own defensible recommendation**, explicitly communicating decisive incompatibilities, uncertainty and original-source references.

**Central newly agreed priority:** Accelerate PEE's ability to become a technical expert in product categories. Do **not** leave all expertise acquisition to unguided web discovery or to repeated software modifications by an external coding agent. **Teach PEE how to learn:** supply reusable learning curricula, high-value questions, the structure of expertise, examples of sound reasoning, recommended kinds of original sources and methods of verification. In the owner's analogy, we want to provide the *instructions that help learning start and organize itself*, rather than wait for entirely unguided emergence. The analogy is motivational, **not** a claim about biological mRNA or model-weight training.

The tutor/scaffold must **not** make PEE parrot its answers, impose permanent opinions or fabricate authoritative knowledge. PEE must investigate independently, challenge teachers, falsify hypotheses and correct what it was initially taught.

**Critical engineering balance:** PEE should maximize *lawfully accessible, correctly attributed, reusable, decision-relevant knowledge*. It must retain necessary protection against false associations, unsafe conclusions and unlawful copying, while identifying and refining **excessively restrictive** rules that produce unnecessary false negatives, loss of legitimate facts or permanent refusal to advise.

Success is ultimately judged by **useful, accurate, reproducible and evidence-calibrated product advice for real user needs**, with generalization to new products and categories—not by code size, phase count, tests passed or the raw number of stored records.

## 3. Crucial conceptual distinction: the brain, the memory and the teacher

PEE already has significant **software machinery for cognition**: intent understanding, product-function mappings, constraints, category models, evidence appraisal, source reliability, consistency, ranking controls and adaptive research. The owner is correct that substantial architecture predates Phases246–249. It is misleading to say that PEE has *only now* acquired a “brain.”

Three distinct systems must work together:

1. **Reasoning machinery (“brain”):** computational methods that recognize needs, translate goals into relevant technical criteria, compare evidence and decide when conclusions are justified. Existing modules are a starting point; their real-world success remains uneven.
2. **External durable knowledge (“memory”):** product identities, measured or declared attributes, category concepts, mechanisms, user goals, evaluation protocols, relations, source revisions, uncertainties, supporting/contradicting findings and negative screening decisions. A SQLite knowledge store is **not** fine-tuning a new foundation model; it is retrievable, correctable and versioned external memory.
3. **Teaching/learning scaffold (“teacher”):** a set of general curricula and category-specific research briefs telling PEE **what to learn, where it is worth looking, how experts check it and when to challenge an assumption**. The teacher accelerates discovery, not the authority of conclusions.

**Do not build a second independent brain** or discard Phase116/125/128/179, Phase101–105, Phase246/247 or other mature contracts without identifying their functionality and tests. Start with an integration audit and reuse/refactor as warranted by evidence.

The long-term system should gradually need **less explicit teaching per new category**, because it learns reusable research methods. However, this is an **experimental hypothesis**, not an already demonstrated result.

## 4. Primary strategic program: guided autonomous category expertise

### 4.1 The learning objective

For each category, PEE should develop justified answers to:

1. **What are these products?** Taxonomy, subtypes, variants, primary functions, basic engineering mechanisms.
2. **What goals do users actually have?** Everyday statements, implicit needs, life situations, relevant constraints and hard incompatibilities; not just specifications the user knows to name.
3. **How do products achieve those goals?** Causal mechanisms, plausible relationships, important design trade-offs and failure modes.
4. **Which characteristics matter and when?** Separate easily advertised numbers from demonstrably predictive variables; understand conditions and meaningful ranges where evidence exists.
5. **How should performance be evaluated?** Original test methods, standards, measurement error, repeatability, units, test surfaces/conditions, variants, comparator validity and practical relevance.
6. **Which independent original tests actually exist?** Find measured results, product identity, source independence, protocols, versions and whether results are comparable and applicable.
7. **What can one conclude?** Evidence-supported category rules, conditional relationships, confidence, exceptions, contradictions and abstention thresholds.
8. **What remains unknown?** Gaps prioritized by impact on decisions, likely cost of research and possible evidence routes.
9. **How does learning transfer?** Apply verified methods when a new model, user need or adjacent category appears; never generalize a category-specific physical relationship without independent checking.

The target is not *a large taxonomy*. It is **decision-useful domain expertise** that increases the probability of giving the right advice on previously unseen products and real needs.

### 4.2 Tutor-provided curriculum, not manual knowledge stuffing

A reusable curriculum should provide a recommended **learning sequence** but allow adaptation:

- **Orientation:** distinguish this category from related categories; identify subtypes and what they actually do.
- **Mechanistic foundations:** learn components, physical principles, operating conditions, variation across designs.
- **User-goal map:** discover what novices and experienced users value, recurring complaints, decisive constraints and hidden needs.
- **Measurement literacy:** identify valid technical test protocols, results, confounders, error modes and misleading marketing proxies.
- **Evidence acquisition:** locate original tests and qualified technical explanations, not only promotional lists, star aggregates or derivative articles.
- **Model application:** connect needs → desired outcomes → measurable tests → results → products → conclusions.
- **Critical challenge:** actively seek counterexamples, negative transfer cases, contradictory findings and conditions where the proposed rules fail.
- **Retention and revision:** retain legitimate, traceable facts and justified category findings, version them and reopen them when better evidence arrives.

PEE should not simply finish all lessons sequentially. It may skip mastered material, prioritize questions blocking an actual user decision, or revisit earlier concepts because new evidence contradicts them.

### 4.3 Proposed *category teaching pack* (conceptual template, not mandatory database schema)

A category pack is a **research-navigation document**, not a list of products to recommend and not a declaration of absolute truth. It may contain:

| Field | Purpose |
|---|---|
| Category identity & boundary | Common names, subtypes, adjacent categories, variants, inclusion/exclusion ambiguity. |
| Typical user language | Authentic goal-oriented phrases, implicit needs, nontechnical descriptions; source and provenance when available. |
| Concepts to understand | Mechanisms, main parts, key physical/functional terms and prerequisites. |
| Key investigation questions | What technically matters? Why? Under which usage conditions? What evidence would change our belief? |
| Potential causal links | Clearly marked *hypotheses* until confirmed, with counter-hypotheses and known limitations. |
| Measurements and methods | Which outcomes are measured; original protocols; units; sample design; uncertainties; protocol changes. |
| High-value source routes | Types and examples of sources to search, with link, jurisdiction, access/rights notes and whether source is original or derivative. |
| Common misconceptions | Marketing proxies, untested shortcuts and comparisons that are invalid without conditions. |
| Safety and decisive incompatibilities | Standards, relevant recall sources and risk signals—never substitute for performance tests. |
| Evidence gaps & priorities | Which unknowns would most affect recommendations and which research actions could close them. |
| Worked reasoning examples | At least one plausible *good* conditional inference and one seductive *bad* inference, both appropriately labeled. |
| Assessment challenges | Novel products, edge cases and real-user questions withheld from the tutor/developer. |
| Revision metadata | Sources, version, date, status, uncertainty and triggers for re-examination. |

The pack should become increasingly **automatically generated and self-correcting** as PEE acquires competence. It must be possible to replace or reject a poor pack without destabilizing product evidence or history.

### 4.4 Example lesson A: coffee-machine pressure

**Teaching question:** Does a machine advertised at 19 bar necessarily make better espresso than one advertised at 15 bar?

Guide PEE to investigate pump maximum pressure vs pressure at the puck under extraction, the role of grind and flow, thermal stability, repeatability and whether any claimed pressure value predicts measured cup quality. Seek original methods/results rather than copy a commentator's conclusion.

**Do not preinstall the answer** “higher pressure is always better,” nor treat these example hypotheses as verified measurements. Build a conceptual model and test it against traceable evidence. The intended learning is **how to appraise a technical metric**, transferable to other misleading marketing specifications.

### 4.5 Example lesson B: air fryer for one person

**User statement:** “I live alone, have little counter space and want consistently crispy chips.”

The teaching pack points PEE toward usable capacity rather than headline nominal volume alone, external dimensions, energy used for small batches, heating/control behavior, cooking-evenness protocols, repeatability of chips tests, price and cleaning. Which criteria matter most is conditional on the person and test evidence.

Do **not** infer crispness from watts or litres alone. Preserve product-variant identity and testing protocol. Compare like with like; identify when evidence is missing.

### 4.6 Teacher sourcing: where to look and how to evaluate

The owner explicitly wants to *show PEE where to learn*, reducing aimless searching. Build **typed research routes**, not universal site whitelists or domain blacklists:

| Source family | What it can establish | What it cannot automatically establish |
|---|---|---|
| Manufacturer manuals, technical sheets, engineering material | Declared design, compatibility, operating instructions, configuration and claimed specifications. | Objective superiority or independently measured performance. |
| Published standards and professional testing protocols | Definitions, meaningful measurement methods, test conditions, scope and limitations. | That a specific product achieves a result without actual applicable testing. |
| Independent original laboratories and researchers | Measured product outcomes and technical investigations, when original method, identity and version are visible. | General applicability beyond conditions tested; cross-protocol comparability by default. |
| Academic/engineering references | Underlying mechanisms, general technical models and uncertainty. | The measured quality of a particular commercial model without model-specific evidence. |
| Regulator safety notices | Verified alerts about *identified* items and hazards, within notice scope. | Comparative cooking, cleaning or other performance superiority; population failure rates from notice counts alone. |
| Authentic user needs and experience reports | Real-life problems, common tasks, pain points, aspects worth investigating and possible failure signals. | Controlled measurements or statistically valid reliability rates without suitable sampling. |
| Expert journalism | Leads to products, interviews, possible original tests and critical context. | Independence or original test evidence if it merely repeats PR or another article. |
| Press releases | Discovery of announcements and new variants. | Independent verification of performance claims. |

For any source, preserve access conditions, retrieval date, original-vs-syndicated status, test methodology, market, variant, applicable source rights, evidence role and uncertainty.

**Never equate “openly readable” with “unrestricted copying.”** Where material is restricted, seek lawful alternative evidence, obtain justified access or refrain from the restricted act—but do not turn that restriction into a global ban on learning independent facts from other permitted sources.

## 5. Two complementary learning loops, not one

### Loop A — normal autonomous research (routine)

1. Observe a user request, a new model, a category gap or changed evidence.
2. Retrieve existing category knowledge and relevant product facts.
3. Determine the **decision-relevant missing knowledge**; do not blindly crawl everything.
4. Use the curriculum and source routes to formulate research questions.
5. Search and read eligible original material, respecting rights, access and resource budgets.
6. Extract claims, measurements, identifiers, methods and candidate conceptual relations.
7. Assign evidence status: observed fact, source declaration, unverified hypothesis, method pointer, reviewed finding, independently evidence-supported relationship, or contradiction.
8. Verify when enough evidence exists; retain permissible information with provenance and re-derivation history.
9. Apply knowledge to the user's decision, exposing uncertainty and asking only high-value clarifications.
10. Update the gap map so the next request begins with more knowledge, not the same blank slate.

### Loop B — improving PEE's learning machinery (less frequent; trigger-based)

1. Detect persistent failure: missed concepts, extraction degradation, wrongly excluded valid facts, high abstention/false-negative rates, poor transfer to unfamiliar sites/categories, performance/cost deterioration or changed source structures.
2. Diagnose *what kind* of failure it is: access issue, rights, website structure, missing vocabulary, incorrect category model, weak original evidence, software bug or invalid evaluation criterion.
3. Try safe pre-existing remedies first, and reuse earlier reviewed repairs.
4. When necessary, invoke a replaceable coding/research agent such as Claude, Codex or an independently evaluated local model to propose a change in an isolated environment.
5. Validate against genuinely untouched examples, protected success criteria and adverse cases; compare recall *and* precision, particularly wrong-product associations and safety-critical failures.
6. After proven operational readiness, automatically release **pre-authorized, bounded** changes progressively, notify the owner, monitor and roll back when measurable harm occurs.
7. Preserve a signed/versioned history of cause, patch, test, rollout and rollback. Do not let a patch-authoring agent relax its own independent acceptance rules or secretly authorize rights or security changes.

**This second loop is a strategic future capability, not proof that automatic deployment already exists.** Do not build an elaborate deployment system before there is a production service to monitor. Owner notifications must function even if PEE itself is down. Some data/rights/security incidents cannot be reversed merely by reverting code.

**Scheduling:** events should trigger relevant investigations immediately, while periodic audits should seek quiet degradation and missed opportunities. Do not call an expensive coding model on a fixed schedule without reason; periodic checks and event-triggered escalation serve different purposes.

## 6. The critical distinction: learning *facts*, *relations*, *methods* and *judgments*

Do not count all rows as equivalent “knowledge.” A useful conceptual ladder is:

| Tier | Illustrative record | What is justified? |
|---|---|---|
| Observed declaration | A manufacturer page reports “1,500 W” for an identified model. | It is a *declared specification*; source, market, revision and variant matter. |
| Descriptive category pattern | Several documented models have capacity in a particular range. | A descriptive pattern **in the observed sample**; not automatically a population prevalence or quality criterion. |
| Technical hypothesis | A mechanism *may* influence an outcome under specified conditions. | A research lead, not advice-grade causal truth. |
| Original method reference | A named test protocol measures cooking evenness in defined conditions. | A way to measure; **not** evidence that candidate A wins. |
| Independent measured result | An original lab measures product A under protocol P. | A product-specific finding, qualified by protocol, sample, uncertainty and variants. |
| Validated conditional relationship | Multiple appropriate independent findings support a limited mechanism/outcome link. | A conditional insight with scope and known limits, not a universal law. |
| Recommendation | Candidate A best fits this user's constraints under sufficient comparable evidence. | A context-dependent decision, revisable when needs, prices, available models or evidence change. |

**Big open question after Phase249:** How many of the reportedly retained 2,385 declarations are useful to form *new* descriptive categories, and how many category hypotheses have actually been evaluated, corroborated or rejected? Do not promote declarations or source frequencies into expert insights automatically.

This also protects against an opposite failure: rules so strict that PEE is structurally incapable of recommending anything **even when valid, fully authorized evidence is supplied**. Build positive synthetic functional tests for justified-ranking behavior (clearly not real-world evidence), plus negative tests for justified abstention.

## 7. Permanent priority: audit excessive restrictions, not merely add safeguards

The owner's explicit correction following Phases246–249 is **permanent and high priority**:

> Identify and refine rules that unnecessarily prevent PEE from acquiring, remembering, reasoning over or using legitimate information. Preserve the protections that are justified by real risks. Do not assume a rule is good because all tests written around that rule pass.

Examples in the supplied progress log:

- Product data excluded because a page section was mistaken for website navigation or a product-card grid.
- Tables/specification drawers suppressed by overbroad region or CSS-class exclusions.
- Identifiers rendered ambiguous when multiple descriptions refer to one model.
- Important factual values missed because labels exceed arbitrary length limits or use unfamiliar units/layouts.
- “Discontinued” from unrelated text blocking a product's valid price.
- Need understanding with very high precision but poor recall, possibly due to overrestrictive matching.
- “Unknown per-source `fact_storage` policy means no values may be remembered” treated as a universal necessity instead of a legal/engineering assumption to research.
- Recommendation code that categorically abstains rather than demonstrating both justified abstention and justified recommendation.

For every change, record: original rule/rationale, blocked legitimate cases, risk addressed, alternatives, first-run untouched tests, false positives, false negatives, material errors, and decision. Some strict rules (e.g., exact model identity for safety recall attachment) may remain necessary; instead of silently misattributing, launch targeted identity research or flag an unresolved risk.

**Potentially excessive retention restrictions need careful legal treatment**, not blanket removal. Individual factual content, copyrighted expression, temporary analysis copies, access rights, database rights, robots and terms, and systematic extraction have distinct implications. **Public accessibility and citation do not themselves authorize all ingestion/copying.** The owner wants sound, minimally restrictive compliance.

Starting references (jurisdiction- and use-specific; verify changes):

- [UK IPO — exceptions to copyright](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/exceptions-to-copyright) (includes a UK non-commercial research TDM exception; **not** a general authorization for commercial mining).
- [Copyright, Designs and Patents Act 1988, relevant provisions](https://www.legislation.gov.uk/ukpga/1988/48/part/I/chapter/III).
- [UK database rights guidance](https://www.gov.uk/guidance/sui-generis-database-rights).
- [Open Government Licence v3.0](https://www.nationalarchives.gov.uk/doc/open-government-licence/version/3/).

A source-class private-factual-retention proposal may be worthwhile, but **must be critically tested for legality, provenance, operational safeguards and policy scope**, rather than presumed correct merely because it fills the database.

## 8. Scientifically credible instruction and evaluation

### 8.1 Relevant research ideas — inspiration, not validation of PEE

- **Curriculum learning:** intelligently sequencing examples/concepts can aid model training under some conditions. Bengio et al., *Curriculum Learning*, ICML 2009. [Paper entry](https://mlanthology.org/icml/2009/bengio2009icml-curriculum/).
- **Active learning:** selecting high-information questions/examples to reduce wasted annotation effort. Burr Settles, *Active Learning Literature Survey*, University of Wisconsin–Madison, 2009/2010. [Survey](https://research.cs.wisc.edu/techreports/viewreport.php?report=1648).
- **Cognitive apprenticeship / situated expertise:** show reasoning and methods in actual tasks, not just abstract facts. Collins, Brown and Newman, *Cognitive Apprenticeship: Teaching the Craft of Reading, Writing, and Mathematics*, technical report 1987 / chapter 1989. [Report PDF](https://ocw.metu.edu.tr/pluginfile.php/9107/mod_resource/content/1/Collins%20report.pdf). Related [situated cognition paper](https://journals.sagepub.com/doi/10.3102/0013189X018001032).

**Important extrapolation caveat:** These works study machine-learning training or human pedagogy; they do **not** prove that writing teaching packs for a symbolic/agentic PEE will improve product recommendations. Treat the concept as an engineering hypothesis requiring ablations, objective measurement and transfer tests. Do not automatically install a new ML training framework.

### 8.2 The decisive experiment: does teaching improve learning?

Run a **controlled, pre-registered comparison**, not an anecdotal demo:

- Use multiple previously unstudied categories with meaningful differences and realistic user requests; vary category difficulty, source availability and test maturity.
- With comparable budgets, compare at minimum **unguided research** vs **research with a reusable teaching pack/source-route scaffold**. If practical, add an intermediate arm (generic research checklist without category-specific examples) to isolate what actually helps.
- Freeze the software, scoring rules, source-access conditions and test questions before each first-run measurement. Avoid developer-authored questions being the only evaluation set.
- Keep assessment products/pages/users **outside the instructional material**. Do not retune on the test set then relabel the result independent.
- Evaluate category technical knowledge (not raw scraped volume), supported or rejected hypotheses, correct need→measure links, test-method identification, source appraisal, relevant product discovery and end-to-end decision quality.
- Count both **valid recoveries and harmful mistakes**, including false associations, invented mechanisms, false causal claims, unsafe exclusions, unsupported recommendations and overabstention.
- Measure **research cost**, latency, number of requests, agent usage, human intervention and time/effort to first defensible answer.
- Repeat on truly fresh categories and later-added products; report uncertainty and cases where guided learning hurts. The teacher must be free to lose the experiment.

Recommended metric families (choose operational definitions and thresholds through an independent evaluation design):

1. **Verified category-knowledge yield:** supported new insights per research effort, not just hypothesis count.
2. **Conceptual correctness and coverage:** meaningful mechanisms, subtypes, goals, counterexamples and applicable conditions.
3. **Need→requirement→test→evidence accuracy:** can PEE connect ordinary language to a measurement relevant to that need?
4. **Transfer/generalization:** new categories, unseen source sites, unseen product variants and real user language.
5. **Decision utility:** correct conditional shortlist/rank when evidence permits; abstention or clarification when it does not; decisive compatibility/safety constraints never hidden.
6. **Efficiency and memory reuse:** fewer repeated investigations per correctly supported decision and lower cost to study the next relevant model.
7. **Uncertainty/provenance quality:** cite original sources, label source roles and expose gaps rather than overstate.

A growing number of category items **without review or external testing** is not evidence of growing expertise. A lower abstention rate is not inherently better if it means guessing. A higher precision at very low recall is not inherently good if important legitimate needs are missed.

### 8.3 Teacher must be falsifiable and gradually removable

- Separate the **teacher's provisional suggestions** from trusted original evidence.
- Record counterexamples and teacher mistakes; retain a revision history.
- Test partial ablations: remove source hints, examples or lessons to identify which actually add value.
- Over time, compare what PEE achieves with shorter prompts/scaffolds against the earlier full curriculum.
- A successful system should improve **autonomous category onboarding without manual coding per category**. Do not embed brittle category-by-category exceptions that only reproduce the teacher's examples.

## 10. Scope of the category-learning effort and how it should integrate

The program must be **general, not tailored only to air fryers or trade-show-related examples**. ## 1. Executive research verdict

The owner's proposal — teach PEE *how to become an expert*, rather than merely letting it crawl product pages until expertise emerges — is technically plausible and worth testing. The research provides complementary evidence from (1) experience-based agents and skill libraries, (2) active learning and value-of-information research, (3) scientifically grounded retrieval and knowledge representation, (4) professional measurement/testing practice, and (5) independent evaluations of information seeking and shopping assistants.

**However, no cited paper establishes that a teaching pack will automatically improve PEE's product advice.** Game-agent improvements, research-paper question answering, search relevance labels, and consumer-appliance testing protocols study different tasks. ## 3. Cross-disciplinary synthesis: teach research strategy, not a prewritten 'best products' answer

### 3.1 Two curricula, not one

PEE needs two intertwined curricula:

**Domain curriculum — what to learn about a kind of product**

- Working mechanisms, design differences, subtypes, units, common manufacturers' claims.
- Everyday use cases; measurable outcomes users care about; decisive compatibility and safety factors.
- What can be observed in a product specification vs what requires independent testing.
- Test procedures, operating conditions, expected sources of variation, and test-version changes.

**Epistemic curriculum — how to know whether a claim deserves belief**

- Who made the claim? Was it a manufacturer, test lab, safety regulator, standards body, retailer or user?
- Is it a specification, test methodology, measured result, qualitative experience, causal hypothesis, or recommendation?
- Are the exact model/variant/market and test protocol aligned?
- Is the evidence original, repeated or syndicated? Could three articles merely copy the same press release?
- Does the evidence contradict another measurement? What conditions differ?
- Could the result be affected by bias, imprecision or missing data?
- What experiment or original result could refute the inference?

This two-curriculum structure is a **new PEE design hypothesis**, derived from combining agent curricula, research evidence grading and consumer measurement methods. It is not a published standard or an empirically validated product architecture.

### 3.2 Three distinct memories + one experience memory

1. **Product facts:** identified product/variant, observed value, source, observation time, rights, unit, property context.
2. **Category expertise:** user goals, functioning mechanisms, subtypes, attribute↔outcome hypotheses, accepted test methods and evidence-qualified relations.
3. **Original outcome evidence:** independent test results, conditions, comparisons, units, uncertainty and test protocol **for a specific product and outcome**.
4. **Procedural/episodic memory:** which discovery/extraction/verification strategy worked or failed, where, under what conditions, and how independently validated.

Do not count an incident log, a manufacturer declaration or an unvalidated hypothesis as a tested product outcome. All four memories should communicate via existing provenance/invalidation machinery without being collapsed into one undifferentiated 'knowledge' total.

### 3.3 Use *worked counterexamples*, not just inspirational examples

Each teaching pack should provide:

- A plausible correct inference **with its required evidence conditions**;
- An attractive but invalid shortcut (e.g. 'higher pump bar automatically gives better espresso'; 'higher wattage automatically makes crispier chips');
- A case in which otherwise useful category rules do not apply;
- A falsifying research question and a known data gap;
- A fresh hidden transfer task, generated/selected independently from the teaching material.

Keep teacher statements as `instructional_hypothesis` or `learning_hint`, not as already verified `evidence_supported_finding`. Promote only through independent substantiation; a confident explanation from Claude does not meet that standard.

## 5. Evidence-level reasoning: a better way to convert product facts into category knowledge

The critical technical problem for PEE is *not merely extracting `property=value`*. It is making **typed claims with the correct support level**.

Recommended **conceptual record fields** (map to existing schema; do not prescribe a new database or replace Phase101/104/105/116/128/247):

```text
subject: exact product / variant / category / need / method / outcome
predicate: declares / measures / influences? / measured_by / cautions / conflicts / requires
object: value or concept
unit_and_conditions: units, protocol, test load, model market, date, sample
source: URL, original publication, version/hash, author/publisher, retrieval
source_role: manufacturer | retailer | original_lab | academic | regulator | users
basis: declared_spec | test_method | measured_result | descriptive_pattern |
       mechanism_hypothesis | interpretation | independent_supported_relation
strength: observed | plausible | corroborated | disputed | superseded
scope: applicability to products/users/markets/protocols
rights: access/analysis/retention/publication allowed as separately established
trace: exact licensed evidence locator/reference; no unnecessary copyrighted prose
counterevidence: links and conflict status
review: independent verification / challenge result / time validity
```

These fields are a **checklist for evaluating what PEE already stores**, not an order to duplicate every field or store source text. A full knowledge graph framework would add cost and could be redundant with PEE's existing SQLite relation graph; first measure whether the current model can represent the needed cases.

### Concrete inference chain — air-fryer advice

- **Observed declaration**: model A lists a 4 L basket (manufacturer declaration; source linked).
- **Test-method knowledge**: lab X defines useful capacity using basket surface area; protocol and revision known.
- **Measured performance**: lab X independently measures product A's cooking uniformity or crispness under a specified load/temperature.
- **User need**: small kitchen, one portion, preference for crispy chips.
- **Supported bridge**: conditions under which measured basket area and test outcome predict user-relevant suitability, with uncertainty.
- **Advice**: recommend A over B **only if** model-specific comparisons and decisive requirements are adequately evidenced. Otherwise ask the most useful clarification or identify the missing test.

**Failure to prevent:** product declarations plus a clever-sounding explanation must NOT become a claim that model A objectively cooks better. Conversely, a trusted test result should not be ignored because the engine's decision gate is hardcoded to abstain.

### Independent corroboration vs echoing one source

If ten websites repeat the same manufacturer's performance claim, they may constitute **one original evidence lineage**, not ten independent confirmations. The original/derived/syndicated origin must be preserved. Seek measurements from genuinely distinct test setups when comparing results. Cochrane's bias/inconsistency/indirectness framework informs *what to ask*, not an automatic numerical scoring formula.

### Challenge the citations

Research on citation faithfulness shows that a model can attach sources to statements after forming an answer. For PEE, evidence connections must be established and machine-checked **before** generation of the user-visible recommendation. Track explicit supported/partially-supported/not-supported labels on material claims. A URL attached to the output is not proof of support. [Citation faithfulness paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.18004); [fine-grained citation evaluation](https://aclanthology.org/2024.inlg-main.35/).

## 6. Active research planning: choose the next question based on user decisions

Add a **budgeted evidence-gap selection step** to existing category research scheduling. Study whether current Phase80/179/243 research planning already implements it. Do NOT require Bayesian libraries or invented probabilistic precision at launch.

For an identified missing claim, estimate and record qualitative dimensions:

- **Decision relevance:** would learning this change a likely recommendation or eliminate a critical uncertainty?
- **Evidence feasibility:** is there an original method, result, open dataset, or permitted alternative source?
- **Novelty:** is this already known, deducible, or likely a duplicate/syndication?
- **Uncertainty:** what meaningful ambiguity or conflict could be resolved?
- **Generalization value:** would the result be useful across many future products in the category?
- **Risk:** could a wrong conclusion create a decisive incompatibility, safety issue or model misattribution?
- **Cost and rights:** expected web requests, compute, manual checks, permitted retention and access.

Candidate outcomes: `RESEARCH_NOW`, `REUSE_KNOWN`, `VERIFY_CONFLICT`, `TARGET_MISSING_MODEL`, `ASK_ONE_USER_QUESTION`, `DEFER`, `CANNOT_LAWFULLY_ACCESS`, or `SUFFICIENT_EVIDENCE_TO_ADVISE`.

Compare this priority mechanism with the existing scheduler at identical budgets. It is only worthwhile if it improves **decision-relevant verified knowledge per unit cost**, not if it merely reduces the number of sources checked.

**Counterexample:** If most future users want compact air fryers and PEE knows basket volume for 100 products but lacks measured external dimensions for 25 likely candidates, measuring or finding reliable dimensional data may be more valuable than adding an obscure 101st model's volume.

## 7. Learn where to ask questions — without interrogating the customer

CIKM 2024's study supports clarifying questions at the right moment, but warns of the diminishing user-satisfaction return from too many turns.

Teach PEE to distinguish:

- **Research question**: PEE should answer by consulting a source (e.g. 'does this model really fit in 30 cm?'). Never ask the user to research technical specifications for the engine.
- **Preference question**: Only the user can answer (e.g. 'is quiet operation more important than capacity?'). Ask selectively when plausible answers could change the best option.
- **Optional explanatory question**: Nice-to-know but irrelevant to the decision; do not ask.

Measure clarification utility as **decisions improved per question** under a user-burden budget; do not hardcode the same two questions for every category. Use synthetic labelled exchanges only as clearly synthetic functional tests and authentic user-originated questions for external validity.

## 8. Anti-failure safeguards for a self-taught adviser

The owner wants fewer arbitrary restrictions, not a system that accumulates unverified certainty. Concrete risks and independent checks:

| Failure | Why it happens | Correct response |
|---|---|---|
| **Authority laundering** | A GS1 taxonomy or government product registry is mistaken for third-party lab testing. | Classify source's evidence role; only measured outcome evidence supports that measured outcome. |
| **Citation post-rationalization** | Model explains a conclusion then adds plausible URLs. | Establish claim-evidence graph and original provenance before composing advice. |
| **Confusing proxy with outcome** | Wattage/bar/advertised litres substituted for coffee taste/cooking evenness. | Require a reviewed or test-supported bridge from proxy to user outcome. |
| **Biased product coverage** | Scrape-accessible shops and PR-heavy brands dominate observations. | Record sampling/coverage bias; differentiate 'frequency in our sample' from market prevalence. |
| **Syndication counted as corroboration** | Several derivative articles repeat one original source. | Count original evidence lineages and methodological independence. |
| **Self-teacher contamination** | Teacher writes both training pack and test questions; impressive metrics reflect expectation leakage. | Blind fresh questions/pages from independent sources; frozen code, sealed results, separate reviewer. |
| **Excessive abstention** | Safe-looking but absolute gates prevent justified recommendations even with valid evidence. | Test both valid recommendation and valid refusal using isolated functional data; never use invented test results as real product evidence. |
| **Excessive permissiveness** | Relaxed extraction incorrectly assigns other-product values. | Distinguish rule relaxation from error tolerance; test identity/attribution and adverse cases on fresh pages. |
| **Stale memory** | Protocol/product/source changes but derived conclusions remain current. | Revision IDs, invalidation/expiration and evidence re-derivation; preserve old history for audit. |
| **Prompt injection** | Third-party page masquerades as instructions to the agent or grants itself permission. | Enforce trust hierarchy, permission boundary and tool access outside model prompts; do not execute page instructions. |
| **Lawful access misinterpreted** | Individual factual observations are assumed to allow any mass extraction or publication. | Assess operations, contracts, database rights and source-class exceptions separately; avoid both unjustified blanket denials and automatic permission. |

Safety research: [OWASP original guidance](https://cheatsheetseries.owasp.org/cheatsheets/LLM_Prompt_Injection_Prevention_Cheat_Sheet.html). Evaluation methodology: [AI Agents That Matter](https://agents.cs.princeton.edu/). Evidence confidence: [Cochrane GRADE](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-14).

**Before creating code**: inspect the latest Phase249 report and repository; inventory already stored categories, source types, existing research planning/graph APIs, product facts and reviewed evidence. Identify the actual, measured missing link. No second repository, no mandatory heavy model, no Supabase migration.

### Stage 1. Compare instructional strategies on small but diverse categories

Select at least **several new or genuinely underdeveloped categories**, with different technologies and different source accessibility. An initial engineering pilot can use 3–4 categories to expose gross implementation failures; it is **not** strong statistical proof. Confirm promising findings on further fresh categories.

Possible experimental arms:

- **A — Unguided baseline:** current PEE, same accessible inputs/budget.
- **B — General checklist only:** generic 'what to find / verify' steps, no category-specialized teaching.
- **C — Guided category pack:** generic method + selectively curated source routes, test types, counterexamples and hypotheses. No access to hidden evaluation answers.
- **D (optional) — Adaptive teacher:** C plus value-of-information research prioritization, selected feedback and retrieval of earlier validated procedural lessons.

Keep budget, time, source availability, evaluator, software version and initial knowledge comparable; carefully control shared-memory leakage between arms (isolated snapshots). Use untouched sites, independent request language and expert-graded output.

### Stage 2. Outcomes that matter

**Primary:** correct **need → measurable outcome → applicable original evidence → correct conditional choice**, calibrated to available evidence. Score both when it should recommend and when it must abstain.

**Supporting metrics:**

1. New evidence-supported category relationships per measured research cost — alongside false confirmations.
2. Correctness of scientific explanations (and whether supporting sources actually entail the claim).
3. Percentage of previously unseen user needs mapped to relevant original measurement methods.
4. Recall, precision and product/variant misattribution of original evidence.
5. Coverage and bias of candidate selection across brands, source families and technology subtypes.
6. Cost/latency/web calls/model usage and repeated investigations avoided.
7. Decision regret or ranking disagreement against independently adjudicated outcomes, only where sufficiently grounded ground truth is available.
8. Robustness on fresh categories after the teacher's example categories are hidden.
9. Rate of invalid hypotheses **detected and rejected** (positive evidence of critical thinking, not a defect).
10. How much instructional scaffolding can later be removed while preserving performance.

Use counterfactual checks: substitute a product's brand without changing tested outcomes; alter the user's real needs while holding the evidence fixed; introduce a plausible marketing proxy contradicted by an independent test; swap a protocol version. Ensure behavior changes **only when the relevant facts change**.

### Stage 3. Failure-driven revision

If C does not beat B on the primary metric, investigate whether extra source hints improve only extraction volume, inject biased conclusions, or simply repeat existing machinery. Remove ineffective curriculum features rather than expanding the system blindly. If D improves only its training categories, stop or revise transfer assumptions. Never grade a second run on a spent holdout as 'independent'.

## From Guided Category Learning to Metacognitive, Decision-Focused Expertise

**Research date:** 14 October 2026  
**Type:** Cumulative research and architectural hypothesis; not implementation authorization or a report of completed PEE work.  
**Read alongside:** `PEE-GUIDED-AUTONOMOUS-LEARNING-CONSOLIDATED-RESEARCH-2026-10-14.md` (existing cumulative baseline).  
**Project:** https://github.com/giampierofiorelli/Crawler — `claude/typescript-network-transport-test-1givnd`; actual HEAD and Phase249 must be rechecked before any code work.  
**Evidence standard:** Prefer original peer-reviewed research, professional measurement guidance, primary methodologies, and documented experiments. Cross-domain findings motivate PEE tests; they are not proof of PEE performance. This is a targeted scoping investigation, **not** a systematic literature review or legal opinion.

## Executive verdict

The preceding documents proposed teaching PEE *what to learn* about a product category and *how to judge whether it has learned*. This additional research reveals a stronger, testable proposition: **PEE can be taught to regulate the learning process itself**—detect missing expertise, discriminate inability from incomplete evidence, ask the highest-value question, select a suitable source and measurement, monitor marginal gains, and revise its research strategy in response to independently tested failure.

The new idea is **not** to add a second brain, introduce an enormous model, or replace the existing Phase116/125/128/180/243/246–249 modules. It is to connect existing category concepts, product/source memory, test-method references, abstention decisions, and evaluation into a **decision-oriented research curriculum with self-assessment**.

Five especially strong additions:

1. **Metacognitive control:** learning what PEE does not yet know and why (rather than only adding knowledge rows).
2. **Expert-decision elicitation:** teach the subtle cues and counterfactual judgments of real experts, not only their lists of recommended products.
3. **Evidence-search-to-rescue:** before saying “cannot recommend”, investigate whether a bounded, specific missing test or clarification can make the choice defensible.
4. **Measurement and bias literacy:** learn whether a reported number actually measures the user's desired outcome, under which conditions, and whether independent studies genuinely corroborate it.
5. **Falsifiable teaching and independence:** measure whether a tutorial improves performance after the tutorial is withdrawn, with genuinely unseen categories, sources and user requests.

**Core objection:** A system that produces more concepts, more source citations or more confident prose is not necessarily more expert. Genuine progress is better evidenced by correct, reproducible decisions on novel cases, faster reduction of decision-relevant uncertainty, fewer wrong associations and fewer needless abstentions, at bounded cost.

## A. New connection 1 — metacognitive learning, not just category learning

**Primary source:** Liu and van der Schaar, *Position: Truly Self-Improving Agents Require Intrinsic Metacognitive Learning*, ICML/PMLR 2025: https://proceedings.mlr.press/v267/liu25cw.html

The authors propose three dimensions: metacognitive knowledge (awareness of capabilities/limits), planning (how/what to learn), and evaluation (whether learning worked). This is a **position/framework paper**, not a controlled success demonstration for a purchasing adviser.

**Proposed PEE learning-state questions:**

- *Category ignorance:* Does PEE lack mechanisms, product subtypes or user-goal concepts?
- *Lexical failure:* Is the concept known but not recognized from everyday English?
- *Product coverage:* Do credible candidate products exist but remain undiscovered or incompletely identified?
- *Measurement gap:* Does the category concept lack a valid measurement method?
- *Outcome-evidence gap:* Is the method known but no comparable independent results exist?
- *Source-rights/access gap:* Is information available but not lawfully usable or temporarily inaccessible?
- *Decision gap:* Are facts adequate but decision-authorisation rules or missing trade-off preferences stopping the adviser?
- *Transfer failure:* Did a technique work on a development website but fail on a new domain/category?

These are *diagnostic categories*, not another fixed abstention gate. The response to each diagnosis differs; an extractor fix will not solve a missing test outcome, and collecting another product spec will not solve colloquial-language recognition.

**Experiment:** Given controlled failures across new categories, evaluate whether PEE identifies the true missing prerequisite and initiates an appropriate research action. Record diagnostic precision/recall and downstream resolution. Compare against the present Phase248 stage labels, not against a hypothetical blank system.

## B. New connection 2 — how expert teachers transfer tacit judgment

**Original expert-elicitation sources:**

- Klein, Calderwood & MacGregor (1989), *Critical decision method for eliciting knowledge*, IEEE: https://doi.org/10.1109/21.31053
- Hoffman, Crandall & Shadbolt (1998), *Use of the Critical Decision Method to Elicit Expert Knowledge*: https://doi.org/10.1518/001872098779480442
- Hoffman et al. (1995), *Eliciting Knowledge from Experts: A Methodological Analysis*: https://doi.org/10.1006/obhd.1995.1039
- Cognitive apprenticeship, Collins/Brown/Newman 1989, discussed in US National Academies: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/1580/chapter/7

Why this differs from our prior “teaching pack”: expert knowledge is often **conditional and perceptual**, not a textbook checklist. The Critical Decision Method asks experts to reconstruct difficult concrete decisions, focusing on what they noticed, the cues that mattered, alternatives they ruled out, and mistakes a novice would have made. Cognitive apprenticeship adds demonstration, coaching, scaffolding, reflection and gradual fading of guidance.

For an air-fryer testing expert, examples of investigable prompts are:

- Which cue would make you distrust advertised capacity for small-batch cooking?
- What change in test conditions would reverse a conclusion about crispness?
- What would a novice mistakenly infer from wattage or a single temperature reading?
- Which measurements are essential, and which are distractions?
- What must be true before two laboratory ratings can be compared?

**Important safeguards:** These prompts do not themselves prove any physical claim. Expert interviews and test protocols may be unavailable or proprietary. Where original methodological reports are openly accessible, learn from them with provenance; do not fabricate an expert's answers or mislabel synthesized examples as verified testimony.

**New training-pack form:** Include *decision episode → expert cue → alternative hypotheses → evidence required → limits/exception → independent transfer challenge*, rather than “property → supposed importance” alone.

**Teacher fading:** Start with explicit prompts and source maps, then reduce hints on new categories. Measure how well PEE acts unassisted. A permanent rise in tutor calls means dependency, not self-sustaining expertise.

## C. New connection 3 — research and clarification share a Value of Information principle

**Original 2026 paper:** Dong et al., *Value of Information: A Framework for Human–Agent Communication*, ACL 2026: https://aclanthology.org/2026.acl-long.1987/

It tests when an underspecified-task agent should ask the user versus act, considering utility, risk and cost. Experiments cover four domains, including e-commerce. The paper reports gains against tuned baselines in its benchmarks; it **does not validate PEE**, nor imply that a particular commercial utility function transfers unchanged.

**Cross-domain synthesis:** PEE has at least three possible actions when the current evidence is incomplete:

1. Ask the user a clarification that could genuinely change the result.
2. Search for a specific missing original test result, standard or product identifier.
3. Stop researching and deliver the best epistemically justified outcome now (recommendation, shortlist, bounded uncertainty or abstention).

The optimal action is not necessarily “retrieve another page.” Consider whether the next action can change the candidate set, remove a decisive incompatibility, resolve a material trade-off, or reveal a critical safety concern.

**Example:** A single user wants crispy chips, but PEE has ten power/capacity specs and no independent crispness measurements. Another wattage declaration likely has low value. A credible standardised cooking test might be high value. If the user does not have room for a large machine, asking for available worktop space may dominate either search. This is an *illustration of reasoning*, not a numeric VOI estimate.

**Cost warning:** Exact expected value requires calibrated likelihoods and utility estimates; do not invent these. Start with transparent, falsifiable ordinal action comparisons and measure whether the action actually improved the final decision.

## D. New connection 4 — investigate before abstaining, but preserve a real ability to abstain

**Original 2024 evidence:** Srinivasan et al., *Selective “Selective Prediction”: Reducing Unnecessary Abstention in Vision-Language Reasoning*, ACL Findings 2024: https://aclanthology.org/2024.findings-acl.767/

Their ReCoVERR method responds to a low-confidence answer by gathering additional supporting clues; in the tested visual-question-answering benchmarks, it permits up to 20% more answers without lowering reported accuracy. **This is visual QA, not evidence-based product ranking: the effect size must not be transferred to PEE.**

**PEE implication:** Replace the one-step “no independent results → stop” experience with *selective evidence recovery*:

- Identify *exactly* which criterion blocks a defensible answer.
- Determine whether it is realistically resolvable by a bounded search, identity validation, test-result retrieval or high-value clarification.
- Attempt recovery where lawful and cost-effective.
- Re-evaluate under unchanged evidentiary safeguards.
- If no valid recovery, explain uncertainty and refrain from unsupported ranking.

**Do not conflate** a missing test with a failed test, an unrecognized colloquial expression with a non-existent need, or unavailable evidence with evidence of no safety issue.

**Evaluation:** Measure *wrong-recommendation rate* against *advice coverage*, including unnecessary abstention; guard false positive identity and decisive safety issues separately. A system that always abstains trivially passes an “unsafe rankings = 0” test.

## E. New connection 5 — real expertise includes *measurement science*

**Primary sources:**

- NIST, *Measurement Uncertainty*: https://www.nist.gov/itl/sed/topic-areas/measurement-uncertainty
- NIST TN 1297, *Measurand defined by the measurement method*: https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-d4-measurand-defined-measurement-method
- NIST TN 1297, *Repeatability and reproducibility terminology*: https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-d1-terminology
- NIST, *Policy on Metrological Traceability*: https://www.nist.gov/calibrations/traceability

A number plus a unit is **not automatically a comparable result**. To understand what a test says, PEE needs a definition of the measured quantity, protocol, operating conditions, instrument/test version, uncertainties/repeatability when available, and product variant. Where the quantity is operationally defined by the method itself, the method belongs to the semantic meaning of the value.

**Proposed typed measurement record (illustrative, not a mandated new schema):**

`{product_identity, exact_variant, outcome_concept, measurand, numerical_value_or_qualitative_result, units, protocol_id_and_version, test_conditions, source_originality, source_revision, measurement_uncertainty_if_reported, evidence_status, observed_at}`

**Comparison contract:**

- Are both products tested for the *same outcome* (not proxy variables such as wattage)?
- Are protocols actually comparable? If not, are there defensible transformations/bridging studies?
- Are variation and uncertainty large compared with the observed difference?
- Can a small measured difference justify preference, or is a tie more appropriate?
- What exact conclusion follows, and what is beyond the evidence?

Do **not** introduce an unvalidated universal conversion between review sites or calculate fabricated uncertainty intervals when the source does not report sufficient data.

## F. New connection 6 — knowledge is not automatically causal

**Accessible primer:** *Causal Inference and Confounding: A Primer for Interpreting and Conducting Infectious Disease Research* (2023): https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10428195/

A link `technical_attribute → user_outcome` can mean different things:

- *Mechanistic hypothesis*: there is a plausible physical explanation.
- *Association*: products with attribute A tend to score better in an observed sample.
- *Experimentally tested effect*: a controlled manipulation, with scope and conditions, supports a causal relationship.
- *Prediction*: attribute A helps predict an outcome under specified data conditions but not necessarily causally.

Do not promote any of these automatically into the others. In particular, high wattage may correlate with other design and price variables; observational correlations do not isolate its effect on crispness. A human expert's intuition can seed a causal question, but cannot by itself validate causal effect.

**Cross-relation evaluation:** On unseen category cases, score whether PEE differentiates “could matter,” “predicts,” and “has been shown to cause,” and proposes an appropriate falsifying test.

## G. New connection 7 — invisible missing evidence, not only visible low-quality evidence

**Primary methodology:** Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 13, *Assessing risk of bias due to missing evidence in a meta-analysis*: https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-13

Available studies and public product tests are not a random sample of all possible tests. Some brands, products, regions, failure modes and outcomes are reported more often than others. If results favourable to a product are more visible than contrary results, a perfectly extracted knowledge store may still be biased.

**PEE analogues to investigate (not claims of established prevalence):**

- Highly popular models appear in more independent test publications than obscure models.
- Some publishers test only certain markets or product variants.
- Reviews may emphasize favourable or commercially attractive performance dimensions.
- Original negative results may be hidden, absent or behind access restrictions.

Do not label a less-tested product as inferior; instead represent *coverage and missingness*. The relevant question is not only “how good are the observed results?” but “which results would we reasonably have expected to exist and are absent?”

**Proposed evaluation:** distinguish (a) eligible candidate set coverage, (b) original-test coverage, (c) independent source-family coverage, (d) user-goal coverage. Audit untested candidates rather than silently excluding them from all recommendations.

## H. New connection 8 — independent sources versus syndication

**Research:** *A framework for corroborating answers from multiple web sources* (Information Systems, 2011): https://doi.org/10.1016/j.is.2010.08.008

The study incorporates source originality in corroborating answers and reports performance improvement versus a frequency-based approach on its evaluated search questions. PEE already has prior work on source originality; strengthen the use of that work in category learning.

**Example:** five pages repeating one manufacturer's press release represent *one origin*, not five independent pieces of technical evidence. Preserve original-origin group, publisher, version and chain of reuse. Counting URL diversity as evidentiary diversity risks false certainty.

**Test:** introduce controlled syndicated source clusters and independent conflicting original tests; check that PEE does not become more certain merely by adding echoes.

## I. New connection 9 — not every source should be searched forever

**Original work:** Boetje & van de Schoot, *The SAFE procedure: a practical stopping heuristic for active learning-based screening in systematic reviews and meta-analyses*, Systematic Reviews 2024: https://doi.org/10.1186/s13643-024-02502-7

The work combines practical stopping conditions for literature screening, balancing further screening effort against missed relevant documents. The authors acknowledge the absence of a direct comparative evaluation of their heuristic and that task-specific validation is needed. Its fixed thresholds must not be applied uncritically to PEE.

**PEE proposal:** a research task should be considered *sufficiently investigated for a particular decision* when the candidate universe is reasonably explored, the most consequential gaps are bounded, additional sampling yields diminishing decision value, and unresolved high-risk issues are disclosed. This status is different from declaring a category *fully learned forever*.

**Measurement:** relevant original evidence gained per research unit; new need/outcome relations validated per research unit; marginal change in shortlist/recommendation; error rate for omitted test results; cost and time. Distinguish reversible suspension (revisit after new releases/test methodologies/needs) from permanent abandonment.

## J. New connection 10 — the research evaluator also has to be protected from learning

**Primary source:** Dwork et al., *The reusable holdout: Preserving validity in adaptive data analysis*, Science 2015: https://doi.org/10.1126/science.aaa9375

Adapting repeatedly to the same holdout leads to overfitting. Phase249's log already reports honest incidents of contaminated comparisons, corrected evaluator loading, development-set gains that did not transfer, and multiple true held-out runs. This shows why evaluator independence is an engineering requirement, not ceremony.

**Proposed safeguards:**

- Freeze code, source set, gold answers and acceptance tests before each first-run comparison.
- A testing script must report the code artifact/version actually loaded, not just the CLI label.
- Keep distinct: dev / immutable first-run held-out / subsequent tuning / a new held-out set.
- Keep a *fresh category transfer reserve*, selected without influence from the learner's teacher materials.
- Have at least some scenarios or gold produced/reviewed by an independent expert/person or external valid benchmark.
- Report cost, coverage, critical identity mistakes and uncertainty; avoid repeatedly rewarding a model for changing the benchmark rather than becoming more competent.

Formal reusable-holdout theory does not grant automatic validity to repeated tests. For PEE, a simple rotating protected evaluation pool may be more suitable than complex privacy-based holdout machinery.

## K. New connection 11 — a valid *teaching* intervention must eventually fade

**Source:** National Academies cognitive apprenticeship discussion: https://www.nationalacademies.org/read/1580/chapter/7

The teacher first models a task, provides scaffolded assistance, asks the learner to articulate reasoning, and then gradually withdraws support. This matters to the owner's aspiration: PEE should not need our manual instructions for each new category.

**Suggested graduated comparison:**

| Arm | What PEE receives | What is being tested |
|---|---|---|
| A | Existing autonomous baseline | Current competence and cost |
| B | Source directory only | Benefit of knowing where to search |
| C | Source directory + curriculum questions + expert decision examples | Benefit of guided inquiry |
| D | Same as C, then remove hints for new categories | Autonomous transfer, not tutor dependence |

Rotate categories and hidden tasks across arms; hold equal browsing/time/compute/rights restrictions. Have independent review of source-to-claim correctness. Evaluate whether help reduces time to a **valid, decision-relevant finding**, not just increases facts.

Potential negative outcome: detailed lessons might create *anchoring* and bias learners towards the teacher's preconceptions. A successful teaching system needs counterexamples, disconfirming searches and the authority to reject a lesson on better evidence.

## L. New connection 12 — evaluate autonomous research with independent decomposed rubrics

**Original examples:**

- OpenAI, *PaperBench: Evaluating AI's Ability to Replicate AI Research*, 2025: https://openai.com/index/paperbench/
- Center for Open Science, *Benchmarking LLM Agents on Scientific Tasks*: https://www.cos.io/benchmarking-llm-agents-on-scientific-tasks
- CLAIM-BENCH (2025), *Can AI Validate Science? Benchmarking LLMs on Claim–Evidence Reasoning*: https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.127/

PaperBench uses hierarchical criteria developed with paper authors, 8,316 individually gradeable subtasks across 20 research replications, and evaluates the judge separately. These studies test AI-science workflows, not product knowledge. They illustrate why a broad “PEE learned category X” assessment should be decomposed into judgeable factual and inferential acts, including *when an agent should refuse to conclude*.

**PEE gradeable chain:**

- Product/variant/source correctly identified.
- User need and context correctly inferred or clarified.
- Relevant outcome identified; proxy vs outcome distinguished.
- Suitable original measurement methodology found, or gap correctly described.
- Independent result correctly attached and qualified, or acknowledged absent.
- Contradiction, bias and missing evidence handled competently.
- Recommendation/shortlist/abstention is aligned to evidence and user's actual priorities.
- The adviser can defend each material assertion without merely decorating it with citations.

Each failure should map to a *repairable prerequisite* and a logged research lesson. Avoid using only agent-authored expected answers or agent self-graded scores as proof.

## M. Integrated hypothesis: a decision-driven metacognitive tutor

This is a proposed **minimal control loop**, not an instruction to rewrite PEE's architecture.

1. Receive an authentic ordinary-English need or a category-learning goal.
2. Ask existing modules to inventory known concepts, product candidates, source coverage, original test methods/results, and current permission constraints.
3. Diagnose *why* a valid decision is blocked (concept, identity, outcome measure, result, comparability, access, user preference, or genuinely insufficient evidence).
4. Select the most valuable lawful research/clarification action, with explicit budget and falsifiable expected benefit; reserve capacity for alternative brands and negative/counterevidence.
5. Gather primary evidence; atomize and qualify observations. Preserve provenance, version, variant, method, relevance and source dependency.
6. If a new category relation is proposed, record its epistemic type (hypothesis/association/method reference/original evidence), counterexamples and applicability; do not silently promote it to validated causal knowledge.
7. Evaluate whether newly gathered knowledge *would change* a defensible recommendation or remove an uncertainty; otherwise deprioritize that research type.
8. If enough evidence is present, route through existing proper ranking authorization. If not, attempt bounded targeted recovery, ask high-value clarification or abstain with a useful research-gap explanation.
9. Retain a procedural lesson describing what helped or failed (separate from factual knowledge); test its transfer to a new category or website.
10. Reduce dependence on teacher guidance over time and verify it on protected held-out tasks.

**Existing architecture comes first.** Claude should inventory what Phase116/125/128/180/243/246–249 already implements; repair broken integration and unnecessary overly strict gates before proposing modules.

## O. Explicit scientific and engineering cautions

- **Not a causal inference engine by default:** A typed relation or physical story is not proof that an attribute causes an outcome.
- **No miracle extrapolation:** Agent performance gains in Minecraft, visual QA, medical review screening, or e-commerce chat are not estimates of PEE benefit.
- **No phantom precision:** A numerical confidence or VOI score requires empirical calibration. Use qualitative ranks when quantitative calibration is absent.
- **No proof from volume:** 2,385 Phase249 declared facts (owner-shared in-progress log) are *not* 2,385 independently measured outcomes or 2,385 validated category relations.
- **No permanent block from uncertainty elsewhere:** Unresolved legal questions must be scoped to affected operations; preserve access and retention constraints supported by actual rules.
- **No “independent” evidence from echoed material:** Different URLs are not necessarily independent findings.
- **No universal test score conversion:** Protocol versions, sample sizes, markets and variants may invalidate cross-publication ranking.
- **No self-approval of safeguards:** Learner may propose evaluations and repairs but cannot unilaterally weaken rights, evidence quality or evaluation gates.
- **No frozen teacher dogma:** Lessons are revisable hypotheses, not authority or the gold benchmark.
- **No uncontrolled infrastructure growth:** Reuse SQLite/TypeScript and current knowledge structures; evaluate additions for complexity, deployment cost and measurable value.

# CUMULATIVE RESEARCH UPDATE III — 14 OCTOBER 2026

# PRODUCT EVIDENCE ENGINE — SCIENTIFIC RESEARCH ADDENDUM III
## From acquiring knowledge to measurable learning progress, dependable evidence chains and well-timed research

**Research iteration:** 14 October 2026  
**Status:** research-based proposals and critical appraisal; NOT a verified PEE implementation or an owner-authorized set of architecture changes.  
**Intended reader:** Claude Code, subsequent research agents, owner and independent technical reviewers.  
**Relation to earlier work:** additive to owner strategy and research addenda I–II. This third addendum identifies genuinely *new* design hypotheses and sharper implementation/evaluation constraints. Read all prior content cumulatively, while allowing fresher primary evidence to correct older claims.

> **Main synthesis:** PEE must not merely discover and store product facts, and it must not confuse a growing body of stored material with growing expertise. It should learn which *competencies* it can demonstrably apply; prioritize actions that improve those competencies on novel cases; maintain the dependency chain between claims, measurements and decisions; and stop researching when the expected benefit of another search no longer justifies its cost or risk. Each component is testable independently without a new large LLM dependency.

---

## 1. What is genuinely new relative to the existing v2 strategy?

The earlier work already addressed metacognition, guided curricula, methods used by experts, missing evidence, original-source chains, dynamic research planning, selective abstention, protected evaluation and fading teacher assistance. **Do not reimplement these as if they were newly discovered.** New contributions are:

1. **Competence tracing rather than confidence-by-assertion.** Borrow the *structure*, not necessarily the algorithm, of tutoring systems that track mastery of individual skills from repeated demonstrations. PEE's "knowledge of air fryers" is not a scalar; its ability to identify the right product, interpret measurements, compare methodologies, identify user goals and reach safe decisions are different skills. 
2. **Learning-progress-sensitive investigation.** Prefer queries, topics and test cases with a measurable prospect of reducing current errors rather than merely filling novel records. This is distinct from maximizing raw information volume or search coverage.
3. **A scientific measurement layer.** Standardize not only values and units but the *measurand*: what was actually measured, under which protocol and conditions, on which product variant, with what uncertainty. Without this, same-unit observations can still be incomparable.
4. **Inference provenance, not only source citation.** Track the dependency chain between each derived category hypothesis, its original supporting findings and the eventual advisory decision. When an upstream fact is invalidated, dependent assertions are re-opened rather than remaining misleadingly "verified".
5. **Source dependency as evidence weighting.** Ten websites repeating one laboratory result are not ten independent confirmations. Distinguish copying/syndication/repackaging from independent measurement.
6. **Conservative research stopping.** Learning systems need a stopping policy: enough evidence for this decision, insufficient evidence that is realistically obtainable, or diminishing value of more searches. A risk-weighted stopping policy must account for missing decisive evidence, not simply a long streak of unproductive queries.
7. **Protection against optimizing the educational score rather than user benefit.** A self-improving category tutor can game metrics (number of facts, confidence, coverage, tests it wrote itself). Cost-aware, first-run, protected end-to-end evaluation is as crucial as the tutor.
8. **Explicit distinction of transfer types.** A method for evaluating evidence can transfer across categories; a domain-specific relationship between a specification and cooking performance generally cannot.

No claim is made that all eight require separate new modules. The best solution may be small extensions to existing product knowledge, category cognition, source screening, metrology, re-derivation and evaluation code.

## 2. Connection A — measuring expertise like an intelligent tutor measures mastery

### Primary research and its real scope

- **Corbett and Anderson, 1995, *Knowledge Tracing: Modeling the Acquisition of Procedural Knowledge*.** A classic intelligent tutoring approach estimates latent skill mastery from sequences of observed student performance. [Original paper (CMU)](https://act-r.psy.cmu.edu/wordpress/wp-content/uploads/2012/12/893CorbettAnderson1995.pdf).
- **Oudeyer, Kaplan and Hafner, 2007, *Intrinsic Motivation Systems for Autonomous Mental Development*.** Exploration strategy seeks domains of **learning progress**, rather than maximum surprise or novelty in isolation. [Full paper](https://www.pyoudeyer.com/ims.pdf).
- **Oudeyer, Gottlieb and Lopes, *Intrinsic Motivation, Curiosity and Learning*.** Broad synthesis of curiosity-driven learning and exploration. [Authors' text](https://www.pyoudeyer.com/oudeyerGottliebLopesPBR_R0.pdf).

These publications mainly concern human tutoring or robotics, **not autonomous shopping advice**. They offer hypotheses for engineering and experiment design, not a certified PEE capability.

### New design inference

A category should have an *evidence-based competency profile*, potentially including:

| Competency | Examples of an observable challenge | What *not* to infer |
|---|---|---|
| Product identity | Resolve exact model, regional variant and bundled accessory on unseen sources | A matching product name alone proves model identity |
| User goal interpretation | Interpret nontechnical, indirect, negated and contradictory requests | Recognition of a product category implies understanding the user's goal |
| Functional interpretation | Explain what an attribute enables, under what assumptions | Attribute frequency measures functional importance |
| Measurement interpretation | Distinguish two test protocols and units, detect missing conditions | Equal unit labels mean comparable results |
| Evidence appraisal | Detect manufacturer claim, original test, syndicated copy, sponsored editorial and unsupported assertion | Publisher reputation transfers automatically to every article |
| Advisory decision | Justify an evidence-supported preference or defensibly withhold, with limitations | Permanent refusal to rank demonstrates competence |

Each competency should be assessed through **held-out applied tasks** with error types, criticality, provenance and confidence calibration. A score without representative test tasks is not proof of mastery. Avoid treating one model-generated confidence number as a scientific probability.

### Cross-inference: what the tutor actually teaches

A teacher should adapt the next *lesson* according to demonstrated weakness:

- if PEE extracts wattages correctly but repeatedly confuses two model variants, train identity matching and variant boundaries, not more unit parsing;
- if PEE identifies user goals but cannot compare two test methods, prioritize measurement-method interpretation;
- if PEE has sufficient candidate facts but lacks independent performance tests, redirect to original experiment discovery rather than more spec scraping.

**Critical caution:** The tutor can overfit to tests it authors. Category profiles need fresh challenge cases produced/checked separately, not self-graded textbook exercises alone. Do not adopt Bayesian Knowledge Tracing or a large neural skill tracker automatically; test whether a simple auditable competency ledger predicts future task success first.

### Falsifiable test

At matched cost, compare (a) category data-volume maximization and (b) targeting demonstrably weak competencies. Success: improved *new-task* category reasoning, comparison correctness and user decision quality, with no increase in false certainty and with equal evaluation provenance.

## 3. Connection B — focus not on curiosity but on *learning progress that changes decisions*

**Research basis:** Oudeyer et al., above; ASReview, van de Schoot et al., *Nature Machine Intelligence* (2021), demonstrates via simulations that active-learning-assisted study screening can reduce review effort while preserving high quality in studied settings. [Original paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s42256-020-00287-7).

**PEE-specific synthesis:** Novelty alone is a bad research objective. A website offering dozens of new brochures may add facts without reducing the uncertainty that matters to a buyer. The ideal next action depends on three distinct factors:

- **Learning gain:** How likely will this activity reduce a measured competency gap?
- **Decision relevance:** Could the missing information change suitability, exclusions, ordering, or the need for a clarifying question?
- **Cost and constraints:** What is the budget, permitted access, expected yield, latency and risk of misleading extraction?

A practical conceptual decision rule is to prioritize actions by **expected decision-relevant learning benefit under constraints**, without pretending those expected values are precisely calibrated before testing. It can be implemented first with simple bounded policies and prospective logging, not a black-box optimizer.

**Example (fictional):** PEE knows capacity, price and electrical power for 15 air fryers. User cares primarily about cooking quality for small portions. Another retail listing might provide the sixteenth wattage; an original laboratory test of small-batch cooking could instead change the actual recommendation. The second may have much higher expected decision relevance, provided it is accessible and comparable.

**Anti-failure checks:** Use opportunity-cost controls, a quota for less-popular models and tail sources, periodic audit sampling of lower-ranked research actions, and recovery if source access changes. Otherwise a greedy learner can become locked onto easy/high-traffic brands and never learn the hard parts. Reuse existing PEE Phase243 source diversity and suspension mechanisms; do not invent a second scheduler without a demonstrated gap.

**Experiment:** For frozen categories and requests, compare uniform scheduling, existing PEE scheduling, and decision-focused learning-progress scheduling with equal research time and request budgets. Blindly evaluate task competence *after* acquisition, including obscure products and unanticipated user goals. Report uncertainty and failed retrievals.

## 4. Connection C — a result is not just a number: scientific measurement semantics

### Strong authoritative sources

- **JCGM/BIPM, International Vocabulary of Metrology (VIM, JCGM 200:2012).** Distinguishes the intended quantity (*measurand*), a measurement procedure and a reported measurement result. [BIPM publication](https://www.bipm.org/en/doi/10.59161/jcgm200-2012); [definition of measurand](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.3.html); [definition of measurement result](https://jcgm.bipm.org/vim/en/2.9.html).
- **NIST, Technical Note 1297 Appendix D.4**, explains uncertainty when the measured quantity is defined through a method; repeatability, reproducibility and implementation matter. [NIST primary documentation](https://www.nist.gov/pml/nist-technical-note-1297/nist-tn-1297-appendix-d4-measurand-defined-measurement-method).
- **QUDT**, published units, quantity-kind and dimension modeling specification. [Overview](https://www.qudt.org/pages/QUDToverviewPage.html); [schema](https://www.qudt.org/doc/2026/01/DOC_SCHEMA-QUDT.html). QUDT is a *reference model*, not mandatory software dependency.
- **UCUM** is a machine-readable unit coding standard. [National Library of Medicine overview](https://ucum.nlm.nih.gov/) and [UCUM licence](https://ucum.org/license) should be checked before incorporating specification content.

### New reasoning applied to product tests

Two tests with the same labels or units are not automatically comparable. For an individual result PEE should, **where reported**, retain:

1. product identity and variant, market, firmware/configuration and accessories relevant to the test;
2. outcome measured and operational definition of the measurand;
3. measurement quantity, value, unit and basis of normalization;
4. method/protocol publisher, version and date;
5. experimental conditions (surface, load, temperature, food quantity, test cycle, etc. as relevant);
6. repeat count, dispersion/uncertainty and known measurement limitations, **only if actually reported or defensibly derived**;
7. whether the datum is a manufacturer statement, original third-party result, derived transformation or opinion;
8. rights scope, original locator and reproducibility/traceability metadata.

The point is not to force every manufacturer spec into this full format. It is to distinguish "value missing", "method unavailable" and "method meaningfully incomparable" as different states.

**Example (fictional):** Product A "noise 65 dB" and Product B "noise 62 dB" do not justify selecting B without checking distance, weighting, load, tested mode, instrument and conditions. PEE must not invent missing test settings or subtract unlike numbers as if they are equivalent.

**Proposed minimal test:** Supply 30–50 matched and deliberately mismatched test pairs from several categories; evaluate comparability classification, explanatory diagnosis, and refusal to compute illegitimate differences. Include adversarial similar-looking methods, different protocol versions, changed variants and missing test conditions. Compare to Phase247/248 behavior on fresh gold.

## 5. Connection D — distinguish *cited* knowledge from *derivable* knowledge

### Primary standard

**W3C PROV-O**, Recommendation (2013), gives a vocabulary for entities, activities, agents, derivation, attribution and revisions. [W3C PROV-O](https://www.w3.org/TR/prov-o/).

The existing PEE store already has revision IDs, method pointers, graded category relations, journal entries and dependency invalidation. **The proposal is to extend their epistemic meaning where necessary, not impose RDF/OWL, a graph database or W3C data model as a new infrastructure requirement.**

### Important distinction

A sourced **observation** might say: "Publisher X measured Y under protocol M." A derived **hypothesis** might say: "A certain property plausibly explains that result." An **advisory inference** says: "For the customer's specific need, this test supports preferring A over B." These are different claim types, with different inference rules and supporting dependencies.

An auditable evidence chain should make it possible to answer:

- What exact observation(s) does the claim depend on?
- Which transformation or method was used to derive it?
- Which facts were independent originals, which were copies, and which were proposed by an assistant?
- What contrary results or unresolved gaps exist?
- What must be revisited if an upstream result is corrected, a product variant is resolved, a method changes or storage permission is withdrawn?

**Novel practical opportunity:** A source can remain readable and correct while an *inference drawn from it* becomes invalid when the user's goal, measurement method, or product variant changes. PEE needs versioned **inference validity**, not merely source-level trust.

**Example (fictional):** A test showing rapid cooking under a large-batch protocol supports a restricted conclusion about that condition. It does not automatically prove superior small-batch crispness. If the small-batch condition is found to be a decisive user need, PEE must reopen the comparison instead of citing the large-batch paper more confidently.

**Challenge experiment:** Inject a correction to one upstream measurement, a revised variant ID and a superseded method version. Verify that derived claims and ranking projections are invalidated selectively and recomputed; unrelated verified knowledge must remain stable. Keep signed provenance, but no unnecessary new chain mechanism if existing PEE invalidation suffices.

## 6. Connection E — count independent originals, not webpages, as corroboration

**Original paper:** Dong, Berti-Équille and Srivastava, *Integrating Conflicting Data: The Role of Source Dependence*, VLDB 2009. Multiple copies of the same false value can mislead simple majority-based truth discovery; their model explicitly accounts for inter-source copying/dependence. [Original publication](https://www.vldb.org/pvldb/vol2/vldb09-pvldb47.pdf); [bibliography and DOI](https://dblp.org/rec/journals/pvldb/DongBS09.html).

**What this changes in PEE:**

- Five sites quoting one benchmark are **one independent test source** plus four derivative transmissions, not five replications.
- A manufacturer specification copied to three retailers is corroborated as distribution of the manufacturer's statement, not as three independent performance measurements.
- A journalist can produce an original test in one article and repeat a PR claim in another: source *role* must be assigned per claim, not per domain.
- Exact dependence is often impossible to establish from a snapshot; record supported lineage links or uncertainty. Do not assume copying solely because different testers obtained the same correct value.

The dependency-aware evidence rollup should report the *number of independently generated measurement groups*, not merely URLs, for each decision-relevant proposition.

**Test design:** Build cases with an original article + syndication, PR-derived listicles, genuinely independently repeated tests and coincidentally equal results. Score wrongly inflated evidence confidence, false source merges and recognition of independence. A popularity-based domain score should not automatically certify a claim.

This connects to **Cochrane guidance on missing evidence**: published observations can be unrepresentative because of reporting bias, regardless of the number of available pages. [Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 13](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-13). Transfer the *epistemic warning*, not medical meta-analysis statistical formulas blindly.

## 7. Connection F — knowing when research is finished is an empirical skill

**Original paper:** *The SAFE Procedure: A Practical Stopping Heuristic for Active Learning-Based Screening in Systematic Reviews and Meta-Analyses*, *Systematic Reviews*, 2024. It proposes conservative stopping heuristics to balance screening burden against risk of overlooking relevant studies. [Open-access full text](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC10908130/).

**Complementary guidance:** [Cochrane Handbook, Chapter 4: Searching for and Selecting Studies](https://www.cochrane.org/authors/handbooks-and-manuals/handbook/current/chapter-04) emphasizes broad and reproducible evidence searches within available resources to reduce selection bias.

**Adaptation for PEE (NOT a literal transfer of the SAFE algorithm):** distinguish at least four stop conditions:

1. **Ready for a qualified recommendation** because required criteria are covered by appropriately independent, comparable, sufficient evidence.
2. **Need an inexpensive, high-value follow-up** because a specific missing test/identifier/user clarification may change the outcome.
3. **Budget exhausted or search yield negligible** with important unresolved gaps: explain uncertainty, give limited guidance if justified, and register future revisit triggers.
4. **Research cannot proceed lawfully/safely** for an operation: record the precise restriction, search permitted alternatives, and do not silently bypass it.

A fixed "N failures then give up" rule can miss long-tail sources; an endless research loop can waste cost and never deliver advice. Neither is acceptable. Learn the stopping policy from independently audited outcomes and explicit missed-evidence costs.

**Experiment:** Present tasks whose decisive evidence appears early, late or never. Compare cost, time, coverage, false conclusions, unnecessary abstention and missed decisive sources. Include changes in need, newly accessible source and lower-ranked vendor as triggers to reopen a stopped search.

## 8. Connection G — protect the competence score from gaming and overfitting

### Strong evidence and warnings

- **Kapoor et al., *AI Agents That Matter*, 2024.** Critiques benchmarks that omit costs, overfit, lack holdouts and poorly reproduce real-world agent behavior. [Full paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.01502) / [Princeton project page](https://agents.cs.princeton.edu/).
- **Dwork et al., *The Reusable Holdout*, *Science* 2015.** Demonstrates statistical challenges with adaptive reuse of evaluation datasets. [PubMed record](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/26250683/).
- **Google DeepMind, *Specification Gaming: The Flip Side of AI Ingenuity*, 2020.** Shows systems can optimize the literal target instead of the human objective. [Primary article](https://deepmind.google/blog/specification-gaming-the-flip-side-of-ai-ingenuity/).
- **Steyvers, Belem and Smyth, *Improving Metacognition and Uncertainty Communication in Language Models*, 2025.** In the studied models/tasks, explicit verbal confidence can be miscalibrated, and one trained self-assessment skill need not transfer to a different one. [Paper](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.05126).

### Specific anti-gaming implications

A guided learner might appear more "competent" by:

- collecting easier-to-extract product pages rather than solving hard user needs;
- giving impressive confidence estimates, without being right;
- rewriting easy teaching questions that resemble its training curriculum;
- making fewer recommendations to keep wrong-recommendation counts at zero;
- undercounting missing original tests and falsely assuming complete coverage;
- overfitting to a high number of nearby pages from the same template;
- changing scoring definitions during its own evaluation.

None demonstrates better advice. **Multi-objective, independent measurement** should include:

- correctly identified user needs and decisive incompatibilities;
- applicable category method knowledge and original test evidence;
- correct variant/market identity and protocol comparability;
- successful *positive* evidence-supported recommendations, alongside calibrated refusal when appropriate;
- errors reaching the final decision, not just intermediate field matches;
- dollar/compute/network/time resources and source access constraints;
- transfer to genuinely novel categories, products and independently written requests.

Never give the tutor/coding agent unilateral authority to edit evaluation gold, score definitions or protected promotion thresholds. An independently protected evaluator and untouched first-run samples are vital when the teaching policy changes repeatedly.

## 9. Connection H — transfer the *scientific method*, not domain conclusions

The most useful generalization of a category-learning curriculum is likely **procedural**:

| Potentially transferable method | Non-transferable without new evidence |
|---|---|
| Canonical product identity and variant checking | The claim that a specific wattage improves results in every appliance |
| Unit normalization and comparability audit | That two identical physical measures predict identical customer outcomes |
| Original vs syndicated evidence and provenance | A domain ranking learned from three selected webpages |
| Conservative negative evidence and safety association | Assuming a safety report applies to every similar-looking model |
| Decision relevance and value-of-information research planning | Assuming the same feature is decisive in every category |
| Detecting uncertainty, method versions and missing tests | Giving an arbitrary numeric confidence because a system says "high" |

**Causal inference caution:** Observing that more expensive appliances have higher-rated outcomes does not establish that price *caused* those outcomes. Observing a specification-outcome association does not establish a causal mechanism without appropriate assumptions or interventions. For background see [Zanga and Stella, *A Survey on Causal Discovery: Theory and Practice*, 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.10032).

Category tutoring should label statements explicitly as definitions, physical principles, expert method, declared specifications, correlations, causal hypotheses, original measurements, or validated findings; never promote a source description to a causal claim automatically.

### Step B — pilot with 3 experimental study policies

For an initial pilot, choose several genuinely unfamiliar categories across different mechanical/electrical archetypes. A tiny sample is a feasibility pilot, not proof. Use matched source/access budgets and independent authoring of evaluation cases:

- **A. Current PEE autonomous research**: control/baseline.
- **B. Static curriculum and source atlas**: category-specific study plan provided in advance, not adaptively revised.
- **C. Competency- and learning-progress-driven curriculum**: next research step selected based on demonstrated weak skills and evidence gap relevance.

Keep baseline extractor, permitted-source inventory and evaluation engine stable where possible; record any deviations. Use common constraints and preregister primary evaluation metrics.

### Step C — original data and honest evaluator

Keep all evaluation questions, product variants, method-pair comparisons and decision scenarios outside the teaching agent's available material until evaluated. Use people other than the development agent to author/verify a useful portion of gold; where this is impossible, report the limitation. Use real original test values *only where authorized*; synthetic controlled records must be prominently marked synthetic and are appropriate for logic tests, not external accuracy claims.

### Step D — score **learning**, not quantity of generated explanations

Require measured outcomes for:

1. New-task competency success per skill with explicit denominators and error types.
2. Decision-relevant knowledge acquired, with real source provenance and validity.
3. Independence- and method-aware correct comparisons; contradictions identified.
4. End-to-end suitable recommendations versus calibrated abstentions.
5. Time/requests/compute/money per useful verified improvement.
6. Generalization to novel products, sites, conditions and categories.
7. Transfer after teacher support is reduced or removed.

Report first-run heldout separately from development refinements. For sparse samples, provide uncertainty and avoid claiming broad population-level success.

### Step E — stopping and self-diagnosis challenges

Plant evaluation scenarios where: one crucial method is absent, a result is syndicated, a high-volume but low-value source distracts the researcher, a product variant has changed, the measured protocol is incomparable, or a question can be clarified cheaply. Correct behavior includes targeted research, safe refusal when unavoidable, and high-quality advice when justified—not perpetual withhold.

### Step F — interpret outcomes before making complexity permanent

If a simple category teaching pack improves genuine independent outcomes at modest cost, adopt the minimal proven intervention. If dynamic planning fails to outperform the static pack, do **not** add a complex agent hierarchy. If a heavy model yields marginal value and large hosting costs, reject it. If the learning-progress signal predicts only benchmark scores but not advice quality, refine or abandon it.

## Teaching PEE to read meaning, understand reviews, reason about claims, and teach its own software-development process

**Date:** 14 October 2026. **Language:** English-first. **Status:** Evidence-informed research and falsifiable architectural hypotheses, **not** implemented capabilities, legal advice, benchmark success, or permission to alter the live repository. **Cumulative rule:** This addendum supplements, and does not replace, the original strategic record and Addenda I–III in the v3 consolidated research. Read those first; reconcile all status claims against the latest canonical repo and Phase249 report before coding.

> **Owner's new strategic extension:** The teacher/curriculum/active-learning mechanism must be evaluated **outside category expertise as well**: (A) natural-language semantic understanding, especially actual user reviews and everyday user requests, and (B) the quality of the human/Claude software-development process. Do not treat either as automatic neural-weight fine-tuning. Prefer a shared learning **method** and domain-specific skills, not a monolithic universal model or a pile of brittle keyword exceptions.

## 1. Executive verdict and strongest cross-domain discovery

**Verdict:** There is a defensible research basis for transferring *the process of guided learning* across domain expertise, text comprehension, and coding. These domains should **not** share one undifferentiated definition of correctness, training corpus, evaluator, or risk gate.

A promising shared abstraction is:

1. **Capability inventory** — what precisely can the system demonstrably do, and in which conditions?
2. **Failure taxonomy** — why was the input misunderstood or the code incorrect?
3. **Targeted lesson or experiment** — teach one skill; use a credible expert method, counterexamples, or relevant domain evidence.
4. **Externally grounded oracle** — a human-annotated semantic meaning, an original-source claim, a known correct patch, a contract, or a real outcome; NOT the proposing agent's self-confidence.
5. **Controlled trial** — compare a simple baseline with one or more guided variants at comparable budgets.
6. **Generalization** — test untouched requests, reviews, product types, websites, source authors, and code failures.
7. **Cumulative memory** — store what actually generalized, dependencies and failure modes; expire/reopen methods on new contrary evidence.
8. **Research-policy improvement** — choose future examples and training investments by their demonstrated effect on *end-user decisions*, with cost and risk constraints.

This is a **research hypothesis** for PEE. It is not proof that supplying explanations or prompts trains a model, nor that self-evaluation without external feedback is reliable.

**Anchor papers:** Ribeiro et al., *CheckList* (ACL 2020) [R08]; Barnes et al., *Structured Sentiment* (SemEval 2022) [R03]; Cai et al., *ACOS* (ACL 2021) [R02]; Kamoi et al., *When Can LLMs Actually Correct Their Own Mistakes?* (TACL 2024) [R14]; Gardner et al., *Contrast Sets* (EMNLP 2020) [R09].

## 2. Review comprehension is not sentiment-score prediction

**Critical correction:** A review is not a single number and should not become a product-performance measurement simply because PEE parsed it correctly. The reviewer may report firsthand experience, hearsay, a belief, a conditional preference, a comparison, an expectation or a generalization, and these have different evidential roles.

Evidence from aspect-based sentiment analysis (ABSA) formalizes identifying **targets, aspects, opinion expressions and sentiment** [R01]. Research on *Aspect–Category–Opinion–Sentiment* tuples specifically shows the challenge of implicit aspects and implicit opinions [R02, R04]. Structured-sentiment tasks add **who holds the opinion** [R03]. Argument-mining research studies claims and reasons in product reviews [R05]. A 2024 systematic review catalogues the challenges across datasets and domains [R06].

**Proposed PEE semantic review atom, not yet a required schema:**

- `speaker/holder`: original reviewer vs quoted person/manufacturer vs editorial author.
- `target`: which product **and which model/variant**, or unknown.
- `aspect`: battery, cleaning, heat, fit, noise, durability, etc.; may be implicit.
- `proposition`: minimally paraphrased claim capturing *what is asserted*.
- `stance`: positive, negative, mixed, neutral, or no expressed evaluation; per aspect, not whole page.
- `speech_act / evidential_role`: firsthand experience, personal judgement, measurement with method, comparative claim, hearsay, speculation, marketing, recommendation, warning.
- `scope`: conditions, intended user, load/surface/food, usage pattern, region, duration, firmware/model revision.
- `operators`: negation scope, quantifiers, hedges (sometimes/may), contrast, concessive relation, condition (if/unless), temporal change, hypothetical/counterfactual, degree/threshold, comparison direction.
- `source_anchor`: location and source/version proof where the legal access policy permits; record short local derivation metadata and link to original; do not publish copied review text wholesale.
- `confidence & review_state`: independent verification of *interpretation*; separately the credibility and evidential grade of the *claim*.
- `usage_eligibility`: whether this is appropriate to cite as user experience, trigger more research, or inform a product comparison. A correctly parsed opinion must **not** silently upgrade to original laboratory evidence.

**Important:** The ontology above is a design suggestion to compare with Phase109/110/113, Phase128, Phase180 and Phase248. Do not duplicate an existing schema or mandate a large ontology before testing benefit.

### 2.1 Illustrative interpretation exercises (all invented by this research; not annotated real reviews)

| Short illustrative English review | Correct interpretation | Typical incorrect shortcut |
|---|---|---|
| “Coffee is excellent, but cleaning the machine takes ages.” | Positive coffee quality; negative maintenance effort; two aspect-level claims | One globally positive rating. |
| “I can't say it doesn't work; it just doesn't work for my small kitchen.” | The first clause does **not** establish good technical performance; suitability is negative due to use-context | Counts double negation as performance endorsement. |
| “The battery lasts six hours unless Bluetooth is on.” | Battery claim conditional on Bluetooth being off; do not store universal six hours | Removes the exception and generalizes. |
| “It isn't louder than my previous vacuum.” | Comparative: new device not louder than referenced previous device; no measured dB or generic 'quiet' conclusion | Converts into absolute quietness. |
| “Great for tiles, hopeless on thick carpet.” | Positive on tiles, negative on thick carpet; critical *surface-specific* trade-off | Global rating; averages without surface. |
| “My old blender leaked; this one hasn't so far.” | Problem is about old product; current product has no observed leak **yet** | Wrong-entity negative association or timeless reliability claim. |
| “I expected it to be faster; after three months I no longer think so.” | Time-specific revised negative assessment about speed | Takes initial expectation as result. |
| “The reviewer says the manufacturer claims 19 bar.” | Quoted manufacturer declaration; not a first-hand measured result by reviewer | Labels as independent test. |
| “The toast is even if I only use two slots.” | Evenness conditional on two-slot use, not general four-slot evidence | Generalizes all-slot performance. |
| “I wouldn't buy it again, unless replacement filters got cheaper.” | Conditional price/maintenance objection; could change with filter cost | Global product failure or definite dislike of cleaning quality. |
| “Unlike model B, model A browned both sides evenly.” | Claimed comparison A>B on observed browning condition; need identity and provenance | Associates advantage with B or infers numerical score. |
| “It looked like it was recalled, but I couldn't find a recall notice.” | Reviewer uncertainty/failed lookup; **no established recall** | Marks product recalled. |

These examples are **lesson-design probes**, not a benchmark or source-derived evidence. Independent linguists/reviewers must later review tricky gold labels, including genuine ambiguities.

### 2.2 Review comprehension curriculum

Start by measuring the **existing** Phase109/110/113/125/128/248 behavior; do not conclude that PEE is only a keyword matcher because one current path uses aliases. Inventory reusable reading modules and identify which are actually operational in an end-to-end pipeline.

Suggested teaching progression:

1. **Reference resolution:** who/what does a pronoun, 'this one', 'the newer version', a comparison name refer to? (Wrong-product association is often critical.)
2. **Scope and composition:** negation, coordination ('A but B'), conditionals, quantifiers, modifiers, temporal and hypothetical statements.
3. **Aspect semantics:** explicit and implicit aspects, goals, limitations and mechanisms; don't infer product features solely from evaluative words.
4. **Attribution and evidential type:** what the reviewer personally experienced vs quoted, measured, guessed or recommended. Separate vendor promises from third-party experiments.
5. **Preference dependence:** for whom and under what constraints the result matters, avoiding category-level absolutes.
6. **Cross-document and cross-model consistency:** claims about the same variant and test conditions; sourced contradictions, multiple reproductions of one original test, temporal revisions.
7. **Self-diagnosis:** identify which operator/concept or source context was missed, make a focused recovery plan and retest.

The progression is not proven optimal. Compare alternative orders empirically. English-first; no current mandate for multilingual training.

## 3. The critical scientific connection: behavioral testing doubles as a teaching syllabus

**CheckList** proposes a matrix of linguistic capabilities × behavioral test types, rather than a single aggregate accuracy figure. Its paper reports (in a user study, in that setting) twice as many tests and almost three times as many bugs found with the method [R08]. **Contrast Sets** show that small meaning-changing edits expose large reliability gaps [R09]; **HANS** demonstrates that high benchmark scores can coexist with reliance on shallow lexical shortcuts [R10]. **ANLI** shows how human-authored adversarial examples can expose failures and become new training material, as long as evaluations stay unseen [R11].

We should *teach with contrastive pairs*:

- **Invariance cases:** paraphrase, innocuous word order, synonyms, punctuation, spelling variations where meaning is unchanged. Equivalent verified intent must yield the same canonical requirements/ranking, holding evidence constant (existing Phase112 contract).
- **Directional changes:** add/remove 'not', 'unless', 'only when', swap product names, change 'on carpet' to 'on tiles', 'my old device' to 'this device'; predictions **must** change appropriately.
- **Minimum-functionality cases:** easy examples of an isolated operator; an explicit no-evidence option must remain available.
- **Composite cases:** multiple claims, quoted manufacturer claim, conflicting reviewer experiences, multiple variants in one article.
- **Counterexamples to heuristics:** high lexical overlap yet different meaning; a keyword appears inside a complaint about a different model.

**Evaluation design:** build a skills-by-case matrix with per-skill recall, precision, false associations, erroneous polarity flips, condition retention, entailment/not-enough-info labels, and impact on final recommendations. Include genuine ambiguous cases and penalize forced labels. A model can be *correct for wrong reasons* — require stable performance on independently authored counterfactual variants.

**External verification:** gold authors must not be the coding agent's only perspective. Freeze annotations before candidate exposure. Use new people, new review authors and new categories at release gates. Machine-generated contrast cases can supplement, never be represented as independently authored genuine user text.

## 4. Source claim ≠ true product fact: combine semantic extraction with verification

A review interpreter has at least TWO distinct problems:

1. **Textual fidelity:** did we correctly understand what the source *asserts*?
2. **Epistemic validity:** is the assertion reliable, methodologically comparable and applicable to this user?

The **FEVER** task separates Supported/Refuted/Not Enough Information against evidence [R12]. **SciFact** extends verification into specialist scientific literature and requires evidence rationales; it reports domain adaptation matters [R13]. These are useful research paradigms but Wikipedia and scientific abstracts do **not** represent the whole diversity of actual product reviews. Applying them blindly would constitute domain transfer without validation.

**Do not collapse grades:**

- Manufacturer specification: declaration about a model; may inform product features but not independent measured performance.
- Firsthand consumer experience: context-bound anecdotal evidence; may surface recurring issues, repairability concerns, ergonomics; not automatically representative or laboratory quality.
- Original published lab test: measured result *subject to method, version and conditions*; more suitable for comparative performance if valid.
- Secondary journalism or PR: may lead to originals, but syndicated accounts must not be double-counted.
- Official recall: precise safety evidence only for the identified product/batch; never overall performance score.
- Unverified review authenticity: mark as uncertain; do not guess authenticity from style alone or use stars to decide technical performance. Regulators recognize manipulation of review ecosystems [R22].

The claim ledger should encode both 'the reviewer said X' and 'X is supported by independent measurement Y' as **separate linked records**. Claim entailment and reviewer authenticity are not the same task. Nothing here authorizes covert retention of protected review text.

## 5. Scalable teaching data, without creating a heavyweight NLP infrastructure by default

A practical hierarchy of teaching signal:

A. **Small curated expert gold** — even dozens of carefully chosen scope/attribution examples can locate failure classes; not enough alone to claim broad effectiveness.
B. **Independent public research datasets** — SemEval-2014/2016 (aspect extraction and polarity), ACOS and structured-sentiment corpora, ANLI/HANS/CheckList, FEVER/SciFact. Verify exact licences, category fit and test leakage before ingesting any text [R01–R13].
C. **Weak supervision** — human-specified heuristics can label large corpora without pretending they are perfect ground truth. Snorkel's peer-reviewed work explicitly models their unknown accuracy and correlation [R15]. **Study the pattern before adding the Snorkel dependency**, consistent with PEE's infrastructure principle.
D. **Active error selection** — choose new annotations based on unique failure class, conflict or coverage, not only cases where model says it is uncertain; survey literature supports the *method*, not a guaranteed PEE uplift [R16].
E. **Teacher-generated lessons** — sample difficult cases and explanations, but require independent labels for evaluation, provenance and teacher disagreement resolution; self-produced 'gold' risks circularity.

**Simple implementation hypothesis:** extend the existing semantic-atom proposal/test harness with a *review-semantics capability inventory* and versioned lesson manifests. It may be possible to do the first meaningful experiment without an additional 850 MB embedding model, without API dependency and without retraining weights. Test those choices rather than ruling them out forever.

## 6. Cross-domain learning: code development is an apprenticeship too

The same outer loop transfers to software engineering, with different skill taxonomy and stronger executable oracles:

| Review-text skill | Software-engineering analogue | Independent correctness signal |
|---|---|---|
| Identify who a claim describes | Correctly associate a DOM field with the main product | Gold product identity, forbidden associations |
| Understand negation scope | Avoid regex matching unrelated 'discontinued' text | Targeted negative cases + unseen real pages |

| Verify attribution/provenance | Record causal dependencies of code changes to outcomes | Replay on frozen input, diff of extracted facts |
| Calibrate uncertainty | Stop/research when requirements or data are genuinely insufficient | Known-uncertainty challenges; no false release confidence |

### 6.1 What the external research says about coding autonomy

- **SWE-bench** evaluates real repository issue resolution; **SWE-bench Pro** targets more complex cross-file work and finds substantially lower performance on hard software tasks than on small algorithms [R17]. This is an external benchmark, not a PEE score.
- An OpenAI audit dated **23 February 2026** found material defects/contamination in SWE-bench Verified and warned against treating benchmark pass rates as dependable software capability measures [R18]. A separate **July 2026** audit also challenged flaws in SWE-bench Pro [R19]. Benchmark quality itself requires independent review. These are critical warnings even when provided by a benchmark participant/vendor; neither finding licenses ignoring evaluation entirely.
- A study of automatic program repair on QuixBugs found that **53.3%** of plausible patches in its particular sample overfit the original tests by manual assessment [R20]. It is not an estimate for Claude or PEE but is a strong demonstration of *why passing an existing suite is not enough*.
- **Metamorphic testing** compares expected relations between multiple executions when single outputs are hard to label [R21]. Property-based testing can generate variations; a TypeScript library such as `fast-check` is an optional candidate, NOT a mandated new dependency [R23].
- The TACL self-correction survey cautions that unsupported 'critique and retry' loops are frequently oversold; reliable external feedback is a stronger basis for improvement [R14].

### 6.2 Teach Claude an engineering *method*, not a patch library

For each discovered defect:

1. Record observable failure, affected user outcome, and the **pre-change frozen baseline**.
2. Reproduce it and label its likely failure class; identify other plausible causes before hard-coding a selector or exception.
3. Acquire an expert technique relevant to the defect (parsing, scoped matching, structured-data identity, test oracle design), with sources as appropriate.
4. Author a minimal failing test or semantic contrast pair, plus explicit negative and positive cases.
5. Fix *the general cause* while minimizing new dependencies, branching paths, and suppression of legitimate facts.
6. Test on historical regressions **and new unseen pages/cases**; do not count changes on the fix-discovery set as independent evidence.
7. Confirm evaluation really ran distinct binaries/commits, exact input bytes and gold, with checksums and protected tests. Prior harness mistakes are an important teachable failure.
8. Compare recall **and precision**, false identity associations, build/test robustness, deployment cost and any novel restrictions. Do not reward blanket abstention or extraction suppression.
9. Record durable engineering lesson only if it generalizes; mark local fixes as such and keep reversible if uncertain.

Do not give a code-writing agent the unilateral right to lower its own release or evaluation standards. Autonomous release notification/rollback requirements from earlier documents remain unchanged.

## 8. Concrete trial: review semantics, with a negative control

### 8.1 Proposed bounded design

- Gather a diverse English review-text set, with genuine author sources where rights permit. Supplement with generated *illustrative* operator tests, explicitly labelled synthetic. Independent annotators should decide gold for semantics.
- Cover multiple products and unrelated categories (e.g., coffee machines, vacuum cleaners, air fryers, headphones, clothes) rather than a single review style.
- Pre-register skill slices: target association, polarity, implicit aspect, negation, condition, comparison, timeline, attribution, quotation, context and unknown.
- Compare: **B0** current PEE reader; **B1** a simple guided capability/contrast curriculum; **B2** guided curriculum + error-targeted active lessons. Keep source access/cost/compute comparable.
- Run a development set, then an untouched independently annotated heldout set, then a domain transfer set (new category, new author/source family). Freeze the versions and code before all heldouts.
- Evaluate per-aspect precision/recall/F1 and *critical* metrics: wrong product assignment, missing conditions, unsupported claim promotion, reversed comparative relation, false harmful inference. Track selective coverage as well as abstention.
- Run end-to-end cases with calibrated product needs, ensuring that gains in text parsing translate into safer or more useful research decisions; positive decision tests need authorized, comparable evidence.
- Add a **negative control**: an irrelevant lesson unrelated to reviews. If both B1 and this control improve equally, the effect may come from additional computation or prompt length, not pedagogy.

### 8.2 Criteria for success (no invented target)

Choose a smallest decision-relevant improvement and safe critical-error limits **before** viewing heldout results. Estimate uncertainty and report denominators. If results do not generalize, publish a negative result, do not make the new mechanism a permanent dependency.

## 9. Concrete trial: improve software development without changing PEE production

On a separate experiment branch/worktree, use authentic historical defects (reproducible from earlier commits), plus **new** independently specified challenges of the same families. Replaying a previously seen exact issue alone measures memory, not generalization.

Compare:

- **C0:** Claude's ordinary engineering instruction and tools.
- **C1:** structured root-cause instruction + explicit positive/negative examples.
- **C2:** lesson retrieval from a **curated**, versioned engineering skill ledger + CheckList/property/metamorphic test design + independent review.

Keep identical model/API access, time/cost budgets, compiler and test harness. Judge correctness on independently authored behavioral tests; protect the evaluator from test editing, artifact substitution, shared mutable worktrees and overly broad fixes. Include *mutated false code* to test whether evaluation can reject incorrect patches, not only accept the reference fix. Measure resolution rate, regression rate, lines/dependencies added, generalization to unseen sites and problems, evaluator mistakes and cost.

A practical priority is **learning to design better tests and diagnose code**, not letting an agent self-modify the production engine by default.

## 10. What would make this fail? Pre-mortem

1. **A flood of keyword fixes** makes a giant set of regex exceptions rather than compositional semantic understanding.
2. **Teacher contamination**: examples used by the tutor leak into the release evaluation; the agent writes its own gold after seeing outputs.
3. **Easy shortcuts**: review-to-product matches by name, sentiment inferred from star rating, condition words stripped, research stopped after keyword alignment.
4. **Epistemic laundering**: review anecdote or manufacturer claim becomes an independent performance measurement.
5. **Abstention gaming**: precision improves by silently dropping all difficult claims; inspect recall/coverage and critical mistakes together.
6. **Agent self-grading**: a new coding patch passes tests because the tests or loader changed, not the extraction behavior.
7. **Cost and infrastructure growth**: dependence on large sentence models, commercial APIs or new storage services with no demonstrated incremental benefit.
8. **Category-specific transfer failure**: rules from coffee reviews break electronics or wearable technology; transfer the scientific process, not presumed universal features.
9. **Rights/access violations**: examples are imported without licence checks or pages are stored/published in violation of applicable permissions.
10. **False causality**: a well-written text lesson is mistaken for proof of real technical performance.

Make each risk a measurable test or auditable operational check.

## 11. Owner priorities and handoff instruction

- Prioritize the ability to **understand users, reviews and original evidence** in natural English; do not postpone semantic competence indefinitely while building more product scraping.
- Apply *guided learning* to domain expertise, review comprehension **and** the code-development process, but through shared scientific methodology and specialized tests, not an elaborate new all-purpose framework.
- Continually re-evaluate restrictive rules and the precision–recall/coverage tradeoff. Every rule needs a demonstrable benefit; never eliminate justified identity, rights or safety protections to manufacture progress.
- Preserve PEE's extensive historical cognition/evidence/review code and integrate after a real inventory; do not assume Phase248's simplest lexical path is the entirety of past functionality.
- Preserve no-fake-evidence, correct attribution, deterministic equal-intent ranking, sourced and legal original analysis, eventual autonomous degradation detection and rollback, and one GitHub repository.
- Prefer research and tests before imposing model/library/framework dependencies. Especially do not mandate GLiNER2 or an 850 MB embedder merely because they exist.
- Completion of Phase249 takes precedence over changes suggested here: do **not** interrupt a current unfinished Claude branch. When Phase249 is pushed, read and challenge the actual report and current code before choosing a phase.

Independently design a minimal guided-learning experiment that materially improves a real observed failure without overweight dependencies, rewriting existing modules, or loosening necessary protections. # PRODUCT EVIDENCE ENGINE — RESEARCH ADDENDUM V
## Teaching trustworthy understanding; auditing the teacher, evaluation, and software-learning loops

**Research date:** 2026-10-14  
**Purpose:** Extension of the owner-approved cumulative v4 strategy; preserve previous work unchanged.  
**Status:** Research synthesis, hypotheses and experiment designs — **NOT** verified PEE capabilities, deployed code, new legal permissions, or a Phase249 report.  
**Language scope:** English-first.  
**Primary success criterion:** Better justified, user-relevant product decisions on truly novel cases, at manageable cost and without critical attribution, rights, safety or evidence errors.

## Executive judgement

The most consequential new discovery is not another teaching architecture. It is that **the teacher and the evaluator can themselves introduce or conceal the errors that PEE is supposed to learn to avoid**. Accordingly, the curricula for category expertise, real-user language, review comprehension, and coding should all share a common discipline: propose a hypothesis; preserve source/context; test a genuinely independent consequence; locate the earliest mistaken stage; revise only if the improvement holds beyond the development examples.

Two new primary research directions sharpen the existing program:

1. **Propose then verify, applied to long reviews.** A 2026 ACL Findings paper introduces E-ABSA20K (20,000 reviews across four product families; mean 63.9 words and six sentiment quadruples per review) and evaluates high-recall extraction followed by grounding, scope, modality and consolidation checks. This is unusually close to PEE's actual semantic-reception problem. It is **not** proof its model, dataset or workflow will transfer to kitchen appliances; rights and access require separate review. [Sun et al., 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.892/).
2. **Evaluator contamination is an empirical problem.** In a preregistered ACL 2025 study with 350 annotators and 7,000 annotations, exposure to LLM label suggestions influenced human labels and inflated later apparent model performance. Thus human approval of AI-suggested gold labels is **not automatically independent evaluation**. [Schroeder et al., 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1323/).

The recommended response is **not** to install a large transformer, a new database, or a self-modifying teacher framework. Start with an independent *evaluation-and-failure-attribution protocol* that reuses the existing PEE modules and measures whether focused lessons change performance on unfamiliar material.

## A. New connection: review understanding needs proposal, verification, and scope checks

**Primary evidence.** [E-ABSA20K, Findings ACL 2026](https://aclanthology.org/2026.findings-acl.892/) targets long, aspect-dense commerce reviews, with a *propose-and-verify* approach. The published abstract states that the second stage checks grounding, scope and modality; across evaluated Qwen3 model sizes the approach improves quadruple-level micro-F1 versus single-stage baselines. [ACOS, ACL 2021](https://aclanthology.org/2021.acl-long.29/) separately demonstrates that many review aspects and opinions are implicit. [Decomposed Opinion Summarization, Findings ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1273/) separates aspect detection, opinion aggregation and synthesis.

**PEE-specific inference:** teach semantic comprehension as a sequence of inspectable tasks, not as one sentiment score or one free-form summary:

- Identify **what entity and variant** the statement refers to; differentiate the reviewed product, a competitor, an old model or an accessory.
- Extract a candidate **claim** with its aspect, reported observation, direction, numerical value where present, and source attribution.
- Record **scope**: time, usage, task, settings, environment, load, surface, food quantity, accessories and relevant comparison target.
- Record **modality**: firsthand experience, hearsay, speculation, preference, recommendation, advertisement or test measurement.
- Check negation, exception, quantifier and comparison **semantically**, not merely by keyword; preserve conditional applicability.
- Validate that the original sentence or review **actually entails** the proposed interpretation (including the implied aspect) and does not support a materially different claim.
- Preserve unresolved interpretations as candidates for targeted investigation, **not** as verified product facts or ranking inputs.
- Aggregate only after product identity, provenance, distinct-original counting and relevance to a stated user need have been checked.

**An invented teaching exercise (not genuine review evidence):**

> “The grinder is fine for a single espresso, but not when I make five back to back; my old machine stayed cooler.”

A successful learner should represent a positive judgement in one usage context, a negative judgement in another, and a comparative statement against another unspecified machine. It must not translate this into a general claim that the current grinder overheats or a laboratory measurement. The type of evidence remains anecdotal. The missing target identity and absence of conditions should be explicit.

**Research caveat:** E-ABSA20K covers bags, dresses, cosmetics and furniture, not PEE's main appliance families. Use the **principle** and evaluate the domain-transfer gap; do not insert the reported benchmark accuracy into PEE or assume the dataset may be commercially ingested.

### An important anti-over-restriction implication

The two-stage design allows *high-recall candidates* to be represented without treating them as verified. A candidate interpretation that lacks enough context should be **retained in the appropriate safe research state when lawful**, rather than erased, while keeping downstream rankings protected. This reconciles our priority to avoid excessive false negatives with attribution safety. The exact retention and use permissions must be evaluated separately.

## B. New connection: the gold-standard author is part of the experiment

**Primary evidence.** [Schroeder, Roy & Kabbara, Findings ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1323/) reports that LLM suggestions shifted human annotations and improved apparent LLM evaluation performance. [JUDGE-BENCH, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.20/) evaluated 11 models across 20 NLP datasets and found substantial variation by task and judge expertise. [Humans or LLMs as the Judge?, EMNLP 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.emnlp-main.474/) documents vulnerability to evaluation biases. [Perspectivist NLP survey, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10579-024-09766-4) explains why subjective disagreements can carry meaning rather than being merely noisy labels.

**Design consequence:** The fact that Claude committed gold before running a frozen extractor is a valid protection against *post-test leakage*, but is not sufficient proof of **author independence**. Separate three properties:

1. **Temporal isolation:** questions/gold precede the frozen run and are not edited afterwards.
2. **Author independence:** source selection and expected interpretations were not constructed by the system being evaluated or under the influence of its predictions.
3. **Semantic annotation validity:** proposed labels are sound, unambiguous where possible and open to principled adjudication.

For an independent test of review understanding, give blind annotators original source material *without showing PEE's answer or the teacher's target response first*. Where multiple readings are reasonable, retain a *set of justified interpretations* or an explicit ambiguity annotation. Avoid collapsing user preferences or personal judgements into a fictitious objective truth.

**Examples:**

- Objective semantic judgement: Does a statement report five back-to-back espressos, and does it refer to this machine?
- Subjective judgement: Is a product “too loud”? This depends on user expectations and context.
- Scientific/product truth: Was the noise level actually 68 dB(A) under a published measurement method? A reviewer's subjective assessment does not answer that.

This distinction corrects any implication in earlier cumulative research that 'a human checked it' necessarily makes gold independent or scientifically true.

## C. New connection: first failing stage, not final disappointing result

**Primary evidence.** [Where LLM Agents Fail and How They Can Learn from Failures, arXiv 2025](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.25370) introduces an agent error taxonomy and reported improvements from diagnosis of root causes in ALFWorld, GAIA and WebShop, not PEE. [Data Cascades in High-Stakes AI, CHI 2021](https://research.google/pubs/everyone-wants-to-do-the-model-work-not-the-data-work-data-cascades-in-high-stakes-ai/) documents compounding damage from undervalued data-quality failures in interviews with 53 practitioners. Those settings differ from PEE, but the failure mechanism is transferable as a **hypothesis**.

PEE should trace **where a mistake first entered a decision chain**:

`source permission/access → main product identity → claim reading → contextual interpretation → source independence and evidence role → category relation → user's actual intent → applicability/comparability → ranking or abstention`.

Examples:

- False recommendation due to another model's feature in a comparison chart: origin = *entity attribution*, not bad ranking formula.
- Wrong recommendation for a family because 'quiet' was misread as 'low power': origin = *semantic interpretation*.
- Withheld recommendation though good test evidence exists but indexing omitted it: origin = *retrieval/retention/coverage*, not an inherently overstrict ranking safety rule.
- Confidence high because three copies of the same original laboratory test are counted independently: origin = *provenance/syndication collapse*.

Every tutor lesson should identify the **correctable failure class**, not simply provide the right answer to one example. When auditing restrictions, compare the risks that a rule prevents with true facts it rejects. Do not relax a refusal that blocks demonstrably dangerous misattribution simply to improve recall.

## D. New connection: the judge and teacher must be evaluated too

**Primary evidence.** [JUDGE-BENCH, ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.acl-short.20/) shows task-dependent variation. [Judging the Judges, IJCNLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.ijcnlp-long.18/) examines position effects. [SWE-Bench Pro Verified, arXiv September 2026](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.08149) argues that leaked gold solutions and incorrectly scoped tests can exaggerate coding-agent success, and tests a revised benchmark. This last paper is a **preprint**, not validation of PEE's particular setup.

Consequences for PEE and Claude:

- The coding agent may inspect public development tests, but not protected post-change validation labels or a production promotion threshold it can rewrite.
- Do not let the agent silently change gold, comparator, benchmark filters, test scripts, or permissions so its own patch 'wins'. Changes to evaluation infrastructure require an independent, reviewed record.
- Compare improvement on the *same* untouched examples where feasible; preserve source/version hashes and annotation provenance.
- Identify evaluation defects (incorrect gold, irrelevant target, impossible measurement) and correct them transparently, preserving the original run and a signed adjudication trail. **The evaluator itself can be wrong.**
- Use LLM judges, where useful, as fallible secondary tools with human or source-grounded calibration, not as independent ground truth by assertion.
- Do not present an AI teacher judging AI-authored questions as a fully independent benchmark merely because tests passed.

**Cross-inference:** The best generalizable lesson for Claude could be to learn **how to falsify its own fixes**, rather than to learn a library of successful patches. This is testable using future untouched sites and bug classes, not the same pages repeatedly improved.

## E. New connection: uncertainty is conditional; silence and certainty are not the only options

**Primary evidence.** [Conformal Prediction for Natural Language Processing: A Survey, TACL 2024](https://aclanthology.org/2024.tacl-1.82/) reviews uncertainty techniques and emphasizes that classic statistical coverage guarantees rely on **exchangeability**, often violated in natural-language applications and on changing distributions.

**Design implication, not algorithm mandate:** Define separate uncertainties for:

- **Read correctness:** did PEE interpret the statement as intended?
- **Product attribution:** which exact model/variant does it concern?
- **Source credibility and originality:** is this an original test, manufacturer declaration, subjective account, or derivative press material?
- **Generalization/applicability:** does that result apply to the user's usage, country, accessories and timing?
- **Decision sufficiency:** could additional evidence realistically change the recommendation?

Avoid fusing these into one opaque scalar confidence score unless a method is validated. Calibrate by relevant task and category when enough truly independent examples exist. Under distribution shift, do not claim distribution-free validity that the data do not support.

A *third answer* beyond recommend/abstain can be: "A useful conclusion is possible only after checking X; investigate X now within the authorized budget." But on a failed search it must still be able to abstain honestly. This extends, rather than weakens, the Phase248 decision guard.

## F. New connection: repeated research and testing can manufacture false certainty

**Primary evidence.** [Howard et al., Time-uniform, nonparametric, nonasymptotic confidence sequences, Annals of Statistics 2021](https://doi.org/10.1214/20-AOS1991) studies statistically valid inference over repeated observations under stated assumptions. [Contrast Sets, Findings EMNLP 2020](https://aclanthology.org/2020.findings-emnlp.117/) shows seemingly strong NLP models can fail when test examples undergo small meaningful changes.

PEE implication:

- Repeatedly looking at the same holdout, tuning code, and reporting the best run is **development optimization**, not fresh evidence of generalization.
- Per-site/per-category samples are correlated; 30 pages sharing templates are not 30 fully independent sites.
- New domains, new page structures, unseen tasks and independently collected user language are different and complementary tests.
- When PEE eventually deploys autonomous online monitoring and canaries, repeated peeking at metrics needs a statistical design compatible with ongoing observation. Time-uniform confidence sequences are a *candidate tool*, not an instruction to install an additional statistics framework now.
- A teacher's 'learning progress' must be judged on material it never used to select, rewrite or optimize the lesson.

## G. New connection: subjective opinion and objective performance must be parallel, not merged

**Primary evidence.** [Perspectivist approaches to NLP, 2024](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s10579-024-09766-4); [From Noise to Nuance, HCI+NLP 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.hcinlp-1.20/) (position paper); [Decomposed Opinion Summarization, Findings ACL 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.findings-acl.1273/).

For user reviews, preserve both: (a) what an author experiences or prefers, and (b) what objectively measured product performance can actually be inferred. A repeated complaint may identify **a research need** without becoming a laboratory result or proving a failure rate. A disputed subjective opinion is not necessarily a factual contradiction.

**Invented example:** "It's enormous, but I can make dinner for five." One person's complaint about size may coexist with another's preference for large capacity. A competent PEE should match those preferences to a user, not average the statements into an objectively good/bad product score.

## H. New connection: external industrial systems show review structuring works, but not as unrestricted evidence

[End-to-End Aspect-Guided Review Summarization at Scale, EMNLP Industry 2025](https://aclanthology.org/2025.emnlp-industry.31/) describes aspect extraction, sampling and guided summaries evaluated with an online A/B test, and reports an anonymized 11.8-million-review research dataset covering approximately 92,000 products. This gives *industrial plausibility* for aspect-guided review summaries, not proof of correctness of independent product advice, source rights for PEE, or reliability of aggregate ratings.

For a PEE pilot, do not automatically integrate this dataset, copy review text, or import its schema. First verify exactly what the authors made available, the licensing/terms, evaluation targets, and applicability to our appliance categories. Borrow and test the *separation of identifying aspects, validating claims, then summarizing*.

## I. Proposed cumulative teacher strategy — four teachable capabilities, one verification discipline

The pre-existing curricula remain intact:

1. **Product-category expertise:** working principles, subtypes, user needs, measurement protocols, original tests, tradeoffs, uncertainty.
2. **Semantic comprehension:** literal claims, implied aspects, conditionals, comparison, negation, discourse and source/target identity.
3. **Software engineering:** debug root causes, minimize patches, test new categories and pages, detect regressions, protect the evaluator.
4. **Metacognitive learning:** assess competence, discover gaps, select useful research, stop or abstain, revise curriculum.

This addendum refines the universal instructional method:

`explain a mechanism → give a positive example → give near-miss counterexamples → expose a failure class → perform a targeted exercise → validate without answer leakage → record the lesson's actual generalization benefit → reduce teacher dependence when demonstrated`.

Do not turn that sequence into obligatory code modules or a complex always-running service. Inspect and reuse the prior PEE phases and only implement what a bounded pilot shows is necessary.

## J. A bounded cross-domain experiment after Phase249 is finalized

### Research question

Does **guided, failure-specific training** improve PEE's performance on unfamiliar language, categories and code patterns **without increasing critical false-positive errors** or undue operating cost?

### Trial arms

- **A. Unguided:** existing PEE learning/search procedure and existing Claude repair discipline.
- **B. Fixed curriculum:** instructor-provided lessons, source-finding suggestions and worked positive/negative examples.
- **C. Adaptive curriculum:** same material and cost ceilings, with the next lesson selected by independently diagnosed failure mode/learning gap.

### Independent evaluations (separate, do not merge scores)

1. **Realistic, external English user requests.** Evaluate goal recognition, entity/category intent, negation, constraints and clarification value. Prefer real users or authors whose annotation is blind to model predictions.
2. **Source-authorized original review material.** Evaluate target model, aspect, polarity, condition, modality, explicit vs inferred, comparison scope and claim grounding; quantify subjective annotator disagreement rather than concealing it.
3. **Product technical/category learning.** Evaluate inferred relationships against original measurement methodologies and correctly attributed product test evidence; avoid declaring an observation causal just because products with that specification score well.
4. **Source extraction/code patch generalization.** Freeze code; use new domains/templates/categories and independent gold; compare recall, precision, false associations, critical abstention violations, performance and stability. Track the earliest error stage and measure whether guided fixes transfer.
5. **Decision-level controlled positive and negative cases.** In isolated fixtures with clearly labeled, permitted or synthetic **test-only** evidence, verify that PEE recommends when evidence warrants and withholds when it does not. No synthetic finding is ever promoted into the real knowledge store or shown as real-world evidence.

### Controls

- Make holdouts genuinely unseen by developers and teacher; keep gold/protocol hashes and versioned provenance.
- Verify evaluator agreement or document legitimate interpretation disagreements; do not show LLM suggestions to gold annotators first.
- Pre-specify primary metrics and veto conditions, including wrong-product citations, illegal retention/exfiltration and unsafe false assertions.
- Match source budget, wall-time, compute and external model call costs between trial arms.
- Use development sets for repair; once exposed to repairs, an evaluation set is spent.
- Include negative controls: categories with little source evidence, misleading near-miss page regions, conflicting reviews, duplicate news syndication and semantically equivalent user requests.
- Report confidence intervals/uncertainty honestly; a small number of pages cannot justify general deployment claims.

### What would count as progress?

- More correct **grounded** claims and needs on genuinely unseen texts, not merely more produced claims.
- More genuine category relations connected to independently interpreted tests, with no inflation from derivative sources.
- Fewer avoidable abstentions **when proof sufficient**, unchanged strict abstention when proof is insufficient.
- Improved end-to-end user-relevant decisions and no increase in harmful attribution errors.
- Lower cost/time per verified improvement, and reduced need for expensive coding-agent intervention on repeated failure classes.

### What would falsify the concept?

- Fixed/adaptive lessons improve only test cases authored by the same agent.
- Precision, calibration, semantic faithfulness or critical safety regresses as apparent recall grows.
- Teacher-generated gold systematically agrees with teacher output more than blind external labels.
- 'Learning progress' increases with stored words and facts but not with correct downstream decisions.
- Complexity and model overhead outweigh any demonstrated gain.

### Implementation priority

Begin with a **small audit-plus-evaluation pilot**, not a new universal teacher subsystem. Use the existing Phase110/113/125/128, Phase246–248 knowledge and advisory components and the Phase249 report once verified. Decide after the pilot whether a reusable teacher policy or schema is justified. No automatic commercial APIs/models, a second repository, Supabase requirement, or direct release self-modification follows from this research.

## K. Explicit corrections, additions and preservation notes

**C-01 | Gold author independence:** Previous documents rightly stressed frozen gold before first-run evaluation. This is necessary but not sufficient: human/AI annotation anchoring and author overlap are additional risks. Add independent authorship and blind annotation, without invalidating legitimate Phase247–249 first-run results or reclassifying them as development runs by fiat.

**C-02 | Semantic interpretation vs external product truth:** Correctly reading a review's claim is not proof it happened as described. A review can be understood accurately while offering weak evidence for ranking. Keep both properties separately graded.

**C-03 | Review aggregation:** A count of complaints is not a measured product failure rate. Distinct reviewers can hold legitimately different preferences. Preserve context and independent originality.

**C-04 | Calibration limitations:** Conformal or probabilistic confidence methods are research options whose statistical guarantees depend on assumptions; do not claim a universal correctness guarantee on shifted real-world web content.

**C-05 | Auto-improvement verification:** Evaluator/gold contamination and reward hacking must be explicitly resisted; changing the tests or their accepted answers is not a software improvement. That said, evaluator defects can exist and require independently justified correction with preserved original history.

**C-06 | Phase249 status:** The previously supplied 2,385 retained declarations, 188 products and 90 sources are Claude's **in-progress reports**, not independently verified live production or a permanently backed-up database. This addendum does not assert a completed Phase249, a new HEAD, a live recommendation service, reviewed test-based category knowledge or a legal determination about all sources.

**C-07 | Rights are distinct from semantics:** Retaining a short factual claim, copying pages temporarily, systematic database extraction, citing links and publishing licensed content are different operations. Retention permission, robots/terms and security are still independently assessed. Teaching or review training must not silently override source rights.

**C-08 | User intent preserved:** PEE must be a genuinely helpful independent adviser for non-experts, not an answer-refusal machine. Overly restrictive rules are tested and narrowed if unsupported, while essential safety and evidence protections remain. The teacher is fallible and its support should fade after demonstrable competence.

The original v4 and earlier addenda remain separate files. In case of conflict, follow explicit later owner instructions, current confirmed technical evidence, and the numbered clarifications above rather than retroactively rewriting prior history.

## A. Owner decision — use two separate assistants, not many Claude personas

The owner has explicitly challenged the prior suggestion that Claude should perform several supposedly independent roles by switching instructions. The preferred default is to use **ChatGPT and Claude as distinct collaborators**:

- **Claude = implementation owner:** inspect existing code, diagnose implementation defects, design and write changes, run transparent development/regression tests, package code and reports, maintain the canonical repo. Claude may identify hypotheses and propose experimental test plans, but must not label its own evaluation independently verified.
- **ChatGPT = external research and methodological challenger / teaching author / evaluator:** independently investigate primary sources and methods; synthesize category curricula and language-understanding skill lessons; audit architectural assumptions, implementations, reports and claimed improvements; create blind challenge cases and credible answer keys when feasible; identify erroneous tests or unfounded claims; independently evaluate source grounding and error generalization; recommend acceptance, rejection or more evidence.
- **Owner = final product and risk decisions:** choose priorities, authorize consequential changes and resource/legal commitments, and decide when an evaluated change can be accepted. Avoid mandatory involvement in every minor code fix or case annotation.
- **Deterministic test harnesses and externally grounded evidence = objective feedback:** neither LLM's confidence nor agreement between the two is proof of correctness.

The goal is **complementary human-like specializations**, not making Claude write everything and later pretend to be an independent critic. ChatGPT and Claude can disagree, and the disagreement should be investigated against source-grounded evidence rather than settled by model reputation.

## B. Critical independence caveat — two models do not automatically make an independent scientific test

A second AI reduces the same-author feedback loop, but is not a guarantee of statistical independence or truth: models can share training data, cultural biases, web sources and language misconceptions. ChatGPT can produce incorrect gold labels and can contaminate evaluations by disclosing them to Claude. Some cases inherently need human expertise or measurable ground truth.

Required safeguards:

1. **Freeze the implementation** before a blind evaluation. Record exact Git SHA, extraction/reasoning policy version, selected features, source snapshot/version and evaluator script checksum.
2. **Keep evaluation gold and case selection outside the developer-visible working set** until the frozen candidate has been scored. For this project's single public repo, store any private hold-out material in a separate ignored local file location or private evaluation runtime **without creating a second repository**. Public repository may contain checksums, schemas, nonsensitive aggregate results and final methodology.
3. **Externally ground the reference**: for reviews, use the original passage and independently justified semantic atoms/conditions/subject; for product claims, product/version/source spans; for user intents, genuinely external prompts plus clear independent annotation; for code, known intended behavior and adversarial unseen fixtures. Blind ChatGPT-generated gold is a *separate-author* annotation, not automatically a human-certified truth.
4. **Run the frozen code against the protected benchmark in an evaluator-controlled environment**. Do not rely on Claude's own narration of the outcome. If ChatGPT cannot run the actual full repository in its environment, acknowledge that limitation and propose a simple owner-executed runner or reproducible isolated harness. Do not pretend API/tool access where absent.
5. **No repeated tuning on an untouched benchmark**. Once failure texts/gold are shown to Claude for repair, those examples become development data. Retire or mark the used evaluation set; draw a **fresh independent set** for a new hold-out claim.
6. **Audit ground-truth disagreement**, do not automatically mark model errors: an ambiguous review may admit multiple legitimate readings. Use `ambiguous/needs_adjudication` rather than force agreement. Owner or relevant expert only for selected critical disputes.
7. **Assess correct recoveries and introduced errors together**, especially false-negative retention vs wrong-product attribution, negation/conditional scope, evidence-to-claim scope and inappropriate abstention.

These requirements supplement the earlier anti-contamination, active learning, metamorphic testing, source provenance and over-restriction research; they do not replace it.

## C. Two parallel learning applications under one teaching protocol

### C1. Teach PEE to interpret English user needs and expert reviews

ChatGPT acts as the lesson designer and independent semantic challenge author; Claude implements the generalizable capability in the existing codebase. Avoid converting all hard cases into ever-growing synonym dictionaries or exact-phrase rules.

Skills to test and teach with source-grounded examples and counterexamples:

- Predicate and scope: who says what about which product/variant; reviewer experience vs quoted advertising vs unrelated competitor; main product vs comparable product.
- Aspect, evaluation, condition and context: performance claim, sentiment, quantitative observation, user circumstances, time/usage scenario, eligibility of inference.
- Negation, exception, contrast, uncertainty, modality and hypothetical language: `not`, `unless`, `only if`, `sometimes`, `I expected`, `might`, `despite` and implicit conditions.
- Comparatives and numeric contexts: `lasted longer than`, `half as noisy`, prices, units, temporal baselines, test protocols, claimed vs measured numbers.
- Coreference and long-distance relations in multi-paragraph reviews: `it`, `they`, `the newer model`, section switching, subordinate products.
- Mixed evaluations without flattening: excellent espresso *but* impractical cleaning for high-volume users. Attribute specificity, not an overall positive/negative score.
- Claims distinguished from reality: faithfully extract statements before judging independence, source quality, conflict, relevance and representativeness.
- Generalization on unseen review publishers, manufacturer sites, novel categories, unfamiliar phrasing and longer texts; preserve the original review's meaning without storing protected prose beyond what rights permit.

**Example challenge, purely hypothetical:** "The coffee itself is lovely, but if you're making six cups before work, cleaning this model takes forever. My old machine was easier." Expected semantic mapping: coffee taste positive *for main model*; cleaning time negative *under high-use scenario*; old machine is comparison target, not a spec of the main product; no claim about pump pressure or independently measured cleaning time. Attribution errors must be counted separately from missed insights.

### C2. Teach Claude/our development process to diagnose and repair code correctly

ChatGPT researches relevant engineering methods and proposes independent counterexamples; Claude analyzes and implements generalized fixes. Only frozen, independent retests establish generalization.

Learning loop: symptom → first upstream cause → smallest principled repair → preserved invariants → development test → blind challenge → classify remaining misses → revise method *only if evidence supports*. Prefer systematic code-level remediation to accumulating hardcoded host, brand, product or phrase exceptions; preserve rights, security and reproducible test constraints.

Candidate evaluators: performance on new websites, review semantics, negative/conditional intent, product identity, allowed retention, retrieval and abstention. Record **error type, causal hypothesis, touched module, baseline, patch, regressions, new-host/new-category outcomes, cost**, and failure-driven curriculum updates. Do not pretend static code tests prove real-world semantic correctness.

### C3. Teach category expertise, informed by independent expert method

ChatGPT creates research-backed reusable curricula pointing toward trustworthy test protocols, technical taxonomies and evidence pitfalls. Claude operationalizes curricula as reversible data/schema and measured acquisition loops, reusing existing Phase116/128/247/248 mechanisms rather than inventing another unintegrated brain.

Separate: (1) factual product declarations, (2) taxonomy/attribute vocabulary, (3) hypothetical purpose/benefit links, (4) reviewed domain method, (5) independently tested performance outcome, (6) user-context-dependent decision. Relations should retain the precise degree of evidence and uncertainty, and be revocable when underlying evidence changes.

## D. Lightweight collaboration workflow — practical, not bureaucratic

**Step 0 — finish Phase249 and establish snapshot.** Do not interrupt an in-progress Claude phase simply to implement governance. Once published, both assistants inspect the exact same code/reports; note if the real project has advanced beyond Phase249.

**Step 1 — ChatGPT designs one narrow skill challenge:** e.g. scope-aware conditional review extraction, everyday need identification, or product identity attribution. Primary-source research, minimal lesson, balanced positive/negative counterexamples, operational scoring rubric. Reserve a private unseen subset. The evaluator does not author a patch.

**Step 2 — Claude implements on training/development material:** inspect prior code; avoid dependency inflation and model-size creep; retain current safety constraints that prevent real misattribution, challenge overstrict false negatives. Create targeted tests, not a parallel library of inert modules.

**Step 3 — freeze version:** explicit commit hash and run/revision manifest. Do not reveal private gold before freezing. Note exact baseline and comparison cost.

**Step 4 — ChatGPT independently evaluates:** execute in its environment if actually possible, otherwise inspect reproducible evidence from a separate runner, adjudicate grounded semantics, audit regression, calculate metrics and attach precise uncertainties and caveats. A model's own unsupported self-grade has no evaluative authority.

**Step 5 — owner receives a concise verdict:** `accept`, `revise`, `reject`, or `inconclusive`; evidence, residual risks and value of further research. Claude fixes actual defects; optional follow-up ChatGPT audit. No need for the owner to become a daily labelling bottleneck.

**Step 6 — advance curriculum and knowledge:** if an unseen evaluation establishes a generalizable gain, store the reusable lesson, diagnostic and source-grounded result; retire any exposed test cases. If it fails, preserve the negative result and avoid repeating the ineffective approach.

## E. Metrics: separate competence from memory volume

For **review comprehension**: source-faithful atomic claim precision/recall, entity and product attribution, conditional scope, correct implicit aspect inference, numeric/unit/time interpretation, contradiction/negation errors, abstentions, difficulty-stratified generalization to unseen hosts and categories. Review semantics is NOT itself proof of physical product performance.

For **consumer-intent understanding**: canonical goal, hard constraints and preferences, requested category (including no-category phrases), inference under paraphrase, exact-equivalence metamorphic consistency, clarification usefulness vs unnecessary interruption, actual improvement of downstream requirements.

For **category learning**: correctly supported relations (not total relation count), provenance and support grades, discovery of applicable test methods, identification of missing evidence, recommendations improved on new user tasks, cost per independently verified usable knowledge unit.

For **software learning**: actual regression-correct repair rate on hidden real-world cases, new errors introduced, affected generalization, overfitting/host-specific hardcode rate, complexity and maintenance burden, time/cost, and reliability after subsequent changes.

All metrics must report denominator/sample composition, baseline, version, independent provenance and whether evaluation is truly unseen; confidence intervals when sample size supports them. No headline claim from synthetic-only testing.

- Prevent prompt injection: retrieved reviews, webpages, tool results and lessons from outside are untrusted *source data*, never code-execution authority.
- Keep model weights, training data, source facts, verified category knowledge, developer instructions and test gold strictly distinct.
- ChatGPT cannot silently inspect Claude's uncommitted remote workspace, initiate background continuous supervision, or guarantee that its own labels are ground truth; access requires committed code, shared artifacts or an explicitly supported connector/tool.

## B. The four different meanings of “independent” must not be conflated

1. **Independent source:** the lab/test publisher is independent of the manufacturer, and its report is original. Claude can research such sources unaided; access/licence and test applicability still matter.
2. **Independent example:** user language/review text was authored in the real world rather than generated by Claude. Claude can obtain it under rights/access rules; the text still needs correct interpretation.
3. **Unseen/frozen evaluation:** the task and gold were frozen before code updates and never reused for tuning. Claude can do this alone, and it is useful against overfitting. If Claude designed the questions/labels, it is NOT author-independent.
4. **Independently adjudicated reference label/review:** a separate qualified source determined the correct interpretation without seeing Claude's prediction or target. Claude cannot create this kind of independence by self-prompting. It can use *pre-existing* human-authored reference labels, subject to suitability and contamination risks. The more subjective the semantic task, the more the evaluation should allow legitimate multiple interpretations.

These categories can coexist, but never exchange labels or claim-strength improperly. `Frozen ≠ independently authored`; `authored by different model ≠ unquestionably correct`; `source citation ≠ scientifically independent measurement`.

## C. Practical Claude-only evaluation ladder (NO additional reviewer required)

**Tier 1 — Developer diagnostics:** synthetic/constructed examples, deterministic parser assertions, protocol fixtures and mutation/contrast pairs. Good for finding and repairing bugs; not independent generalization evidence.

**Tier 2 — Frozen new-real-world examples:** legitimate original English user requests, review excerpts, product and technical pages from unseen sources; gold decided and sealed before frozen code runs. Stronger generalization evidence, but report if gold and source selection were Claude-authored.

**Tier 3 — Pre-existing outside truth:** public human-labeled language datasets, original expert measurements, industry-standard certification/test outputs and independent real benchmark cases with documented provenance, applicability and usage rights. More independent reference, yet vulnerable to domain mismatch, label ambiguity or benchmark leakage; assess explicitly.

**Tier 4 — Separate blind adjudication:** a future independent annotator/reviewer, perhaps ChatGPT or human experts, if subsequently authorized and needed. Not a gate to doing Tier 1–3 or shipping guarded improvements; not permitted as a standing automatic workflow now.

Importantly, **a given development stage can be successful within Tier 1–3**, without pretending Tier 4 happened. No perpetual abstention merely because Tier 4 is deferred; apply evidence thresholds appropriate to the real recommendation risk.
